" I don't think I can do it. "
Each word small as grains of sand trickling from a parched throat. She squeezed his palm, then pulled him forward.
A vast, lush green field suddenly opened right before his eyes. White clouds drifted beneath a jade blue sky, with nothing holding them back, and nothing forcing them to drift. He looked up at that boundless expanse. His chest felt so light it was strange, so distant that he could find no word to name it.
Her steps quickened. Her slender hand still steadfastly led him on.
" Come over there with me. "
Her smile was like the last bit of sunlight left on a late, late afternoon. Still that familiar haughty air, but no more heavy worries, no more faded gold, no more of the wary anxiety she always carried. Only her.
Tiffany Kristen.
He carefully took in every step she made across the grass, trying to carve it in as deeply as he could. The scent of her hair lingered in the breeze that had just passed, a bit of mortal longing he selfishly wanted to keep for himself. Like the time they walked side by side in Manlanier. Only this time, his heart stopped making up excuses.
They crossed a carpet of pure white flowers. Each stem swayed gently in the afternoon light, not a single petal left behind. Her hand. Her eyes. Nothing had ever held his attention so much.
" Where are you taking me? "
" Almost there. Wait for me a little. "
He stopped asking, simply following each step she left on the grass. Below the hillside ahead, a flock of ownerless sheep wandered leisurely beneath the endless green. They bent to graze on clumps of wild grass, now and then a few lazily lifting their heads to look at the pale sunlight, not the least bit bothered by the two strangers who had just arrived.
Kristin let go of his hand and ran carefree straight into the middle of the flock.
He stopped. He had never seen her so at ease. No more burdens, no more of the troubles of the human world closing in. Only a carefree woman in the middle of a wild flock.
" Come here with me. "
" I don't like them. "
She frowned.
" You will. "
He stood stubbornly still. She fixed her eyes on him, and he glared back obstinately. But holding out against those eyes was harder than he thought. In the end, the one who had refused to bow even before the sky was the one who gave in and stepped forward.
Kristin was holding a little lamb in her arms. Its ivory fleece curled tight into little tufts, and it lay docile between her arms.
" Cute, isn't it? "
He glanced at the animal.
" No. "
" You really are the driest person I've ever met. "
She was always like that, grumbling, scolding him for things he had never thought he needed to fix. Strangely, he did not mind it at all. Reproach from these lips had never carried the weight of a curse.
She bent down slightly.
" Try touching it? "
He lowered his eyes. A little hesitation caught on his eyelids. He awkwardly reached toward the curly fleece.
Soft.
The little lamb jerked its head up.
Baaaa-a.
He snatched his hand back, so fast it was as if he had never meant to touch it at all. Kristin burst out laughing. Not the thin, faint smile he had seen at Mother of Mothers. She laughed out loud, so much she had to lean over and hold the lamb tight to keep it from slipping out of her arms.
" See? It won't like you if you keep being so wary. "
He frowned.
" I— "
He stopped, then hurried to correct himself.
" I'm not being wary. "
She pointed her eyes at his hand, still hovering stiffly in midair.
" Then why is it shaking? "
Caught off guard, he pulled his hand back into a fist.
" I'm not afraid. "
" I never said you were afraid. "
He fell silent. A clumsy lie, exposing exactly what it wanted to hide, and even more foolish for being caught before he could even finish twisting his tongue around it. Kristin took his wrist and, little by little, brought that hand back toward the lamb.
" Gently now. What matters is that you don't scare it. "
" I told you I'm not scared. "
" I'm talking about the lamb. "
The corners of her lips curved in triumph. He went completely silent. He could not understand by what magic this woman could still so easily turn him into a fool with only a few short words.
She pressed his hand onto the white fleece. This time the animal did not cry out. He stroked it lightly. Once. Then once more.
Until now, most of the creatures that had ever stood within his reach had either fled in terror, or screamed, or prostrated themselves. He had grown far too used to those pitiful sounds. Yet this tiny creature only lay still, even nuzzling its head into his palm. A strange feeling crept in between his rough fingers.
Peace.
Something he had once scorned, thinking it was only for those who had not yet grasped how cruel this world could be.
" You did it. It's not hard. "
He did not answer, his hand still clumsily stroking the soft fleece.
Then came a rustling from behind. Another sheep was trotting toward them. It looked like none of the others in the flock. Jet-black fleece, blacker than the shadows stretching long beneath the afternoon sun. Two eyes red as rubies sat sunken in its gaunt face. With every step it took, the white sheep scattered to either side, leaving a wide empty space around it. As if it had never belonged here.
Kristin set the white lamb down on the grass and slowly walked toward the newcomer. The black sheep stopped right before the tips of her toes. It did not flee, nor did it seem afraid. She knelt and lifted it up.
He watched quietly. Her hand tenderly stroked along the pitch-black fleece, her fingertips stopping now and then, catching. Beneath the thick fleece, old scars rose beneath the skin. One. Then another. They crossed one another, running all over its small body. Kristin said nothing, only kept stroking. The sheep lay still in her arms.
After a while, her eyes still not leaving that different lamb, she asked.
" There's one thing you still haven't told me. "
" What? "
" What are you looking for here? "
His throat went bitter. The question echoed back from a cramped tavern thick with smoke, where a little of the dying daylight crept through a battered window, where there was a sprig of bland, cold broccoli, and the woman who had once sat quietly across from him. Back then he had been silent. This time he did not need to think.
" I think I'm looking for you, Kristin. "
She was silent, then gently shook her dark head.
" Then you still don't know what you need to look for. "
He frowned.
" Then tell me. What do I need to look for? "
She shrugged, then looked up at him as if the question were utterly absurd.
" How would I know. "
" You— "
He cut himself off. She laughed clearly, then turned fully around, her slender finger pointing toward the hazy horizon. At the very end of the green grass, where the evening light was wearily sinking, a hill stood alone. On its top, an ancient, gnarled olive tree bent, bowing to the wind. No forest grew around it, no fortress, not a single roof. Only it, tall enough to look out over the whole field below. Its branches reached out in all four directions, every tiny leaf gleaming silver in the sunset.
" All you have to do is walk forward. "
He followed her gaze. Between where he stood and the distant hill there was not a single path. No footprints, no wooden posts to point the way. Only a vast stretch of wild grass.
" I don't know where to put my feet. "
" Anywhere you can step. "
He kept looking at the wild ground before him. No hand from the sky pointing the way. No bloody prophecy calling to him. No forbidden name forcing him to become anything. Only wild grass, and a pair of bare feet bearing a thousand scratches.
Kristin set the black sheep back down on the ground. Its four little legs stood hesitating beside her for a moment. She stroked the fleece on its head one last time, then the animal quietly turned away. It began to run. It did not go back toward the white flock, did not care about the empty space they always drew around it. The scars beneath its fleece would always remain, but its four thin legs refused to stand still. It ran headlong across the field, smaller and smaller, until it was only a black dot beneath the setting sun.
Kristin quietly watched it go. Then she reached out and held his hand tight. The evening light glimmered in her eyes.
" I believe you can do it. "
The warmth of her palm pressed into his skin, spreading through every joint, creeping through every finger, like tiny sparks trying to light a road that had never existed in this world. In that fragile moment, he did not care about the old olive tree, nor did he wish to know what lay on the other side of the field. He only wanted this small hand to keep resting in his.
" I don't think I can do it. "
She did not blame him. She only squeezed his hand a little tighter. A very gentle squeeze.
Just enough for everything to shatter.

He blinked.
The sky of Cvstodia was still ablaze. Red lightning tore at every layer of cloud. Snow began to fall, mixed with ash. A small white flake landed on their two hands still entwined, then melted.
Kristin still lay motionless before him. Her small hand still rested in his.
Only the warmth was gone.
Cold.
Colder than any of her brief naps in his arms.
He sat where he was, longer than any length of time he had ever been willing to spend on this mortal world. He had never let time pass so meaninglessly, but now there was no reason left to excuse it. Only the snowflakes falling ceaselessly onto her hair. A few caught on her curled lashes. There would be no more blinks to brush them away.
He reached out and slowly brushed them from her cheek. His fingers stopped on her smooth skin.
Colder than before.
No miracle would save her, nor any Miracle redeem his soul. No more reproaches from the lips before him. No more of the woman who kept reminding him every time he backed away.
The hand that had trembled a moment ago had stopped trembling.
He bent down, one arm sliding beneath her back, the other supporting her knees, then lifted her up.
Light.
Lighter than he remembered. Or perhaps her last thoughts had also left that body.
The ash-grey wings burst open in the falling snow. He looked around. The giant roots of The Root of Sin still crawled ceaselessly deep beneath the Endless Dusk. The trees were only bare lumps of charcoal. And everywhere were human bodies trampled by the Miracle. Flesh could be torn from bone. Blood could run dry. But life was still not allowed to end, and their pain simply went on existing.
He would not leave Kristin lying in the middle of this chaos.
His wings folded around the body in his arms, then shot straight into the snowstorm. The freezing wind lashed across his face. He held her tight against himself, as if he did not want the cold to touch that small body ever again.
His wings carried him back to the place he never wanted to return to. Had he been alone, he would never have gone back.
Mother of Mothers.
The spires of the church slowly appeared behind the thick haze of snow and ash. It no longer looked as it had in days past. Part of the dome he had once smashed, the bell tower warped with a corner broken away. But there was still firelight flickering through the cracks. Those still whole had found their way back to this ruin. Tables and chairs, planks of wood, broken bricks, anything human strength could lift had become the last barricade for the miserable lives inside.
Because right above the dome, the Darksiders circled as if they had sniffed out the smell of fear below. The bat-winged creatures bared their claws and swooped down again and again, clawing the barricade apart. The people inside fought back with a few sharpened wooden stakes. Screams echoed across the sky.
He did not bother to care. To him right now, the people and the monsters were not so different.
He raised one hand forward, the other still holding Kristin tight. Drops of blood gathered at his fingertips.
BOOM.
The sphere of blood shot straight into the swarm of bats. Piercing. Ripping. Crushing the grotesque bodies that had crawled up from the cracks. But it did not stop. As if it had eyes, it turned, chasing the monsters not yet fully dead, until the last slimy chunks of flesh fell to the ground. Then it changed direction once more and shot straight into the dome.
Stone and brick burst apart.
He wrapped his wings around himself and plunged through the dust, landing hard in the great hall. A white rain of plaster dust crashed down, snuffing out the candles still half burned. Those sheltering below screamed in terror, a few pointing their wooden spears at him. Only when the dust slowly cleared did they stumble back, stunned. Before them was not some desperate soul seeking shelter. A pair of ash-grey wings covered in cold snow. In his arms, a woman who had fallen into eternal sleep.
A few people lowered their weapons with trembling hands. Some fell to their knees before that winged figure. Only a priest stepped forward boldly.
" How dare you destroy this holy place? "
He kept striding on, ignoring the whispers all around.
" Hey! Do you know what these wretched people had to pay to keep it? "
He stopped and glanced over indifferently. The priest swallowed hard before that gaze.
" I thought this place was abandoned. "
No one dared answer. The cold words fell into the middle of the great hall. He glanced over the patched planks boarding up the doors, the prayer pews torn apart to make barricades, the desperate faces counting each bead of the rosaries in their hands. Who knew how long they had turned their backs on this place. And yet when the sky came crashing down, they had come running back in panic.
A bitter thought crept into his shattered heart. He was no different from them. He too had only come back here when nowhere out there could keep the thing he cherished most.
He walked on, leaving the people behind, plodding silently past the scattered pews and into the familiar dark corridor.
Step by step.
Each step heavy.
Fragment after fragment of memory slid across his sight. She had once rested on his back right here.
Step by step. Slower.
The rambling stories that had once left her lips, gently brushing his ear.
Step by step. Still not stopping.
The trivial thoughts he had buried, which he should have told her. It was too late now for such regrets. He had once thought this corridor was endless, or that it was only because that troublesome woman would not let the silence last a single moment. Whatever it was, now he only felt the ache of it being so short.
At the end of the corridor, the cold table still stood. He recognized it. The place where he had once wrapped coarse layers of cloth around her shoulder. Also the place where her fingers had once feebly held him back.
He bent down and gently laid Kristin on the table, then stared blankly at that face. His clumsy hand brushed a tangled lock of hair aside. Her arms had fallen askew from her body; he lifted them and laid them neatly across her stomach, her small fingers resting one over the other. Snow from beyond the dome still blew in through the hole. A flake melted on her lips.
He drew the cloth over her. He did not want her to be cold because of anything anymore. Even though he knew she could no longer feel the cold.
His hand lingered on the edge of the cloth for a very long time. At last he let go.
He turned his back on her.
One step.
Then another.
Still no hand held him back.
He knew she was at rest. Knew it very well. But pain never listens to what he knows. He still wished that grip of her hand could hold him back.
One more step. Still waiting for that warmth. Both arms hanging limp. Not a call. Not a tug. He did not dare look back. Throughout the church there were only lingering, wretched sobs, useless prayers. No sound he wanted to hear. He only waited for an echo behind his back. Waited for that familiar hand to hold him back.
He squeezed his eyes shut. The emptiness bored deep into his worn-out soul.
There was no miracle for him. There was no grace that belonged to him. Only the clawing, and the filthy stains of his own soul.
He walked out of the room.
In the middle of the corridor, something dark lay sprawled on the stone floor, right where he had taken it off. The coat she had gone to the trouble of buying him. He picked it up. The fabric still carried the scent of Manlanier's dust. He threw it over his shoulders. Inside its fold, his fingers touched a bruised apple and four ice-cold Chromee, still lying in their old place.
He drew the collar tight. Then walked on.

He stood beneath the dome he himself had just smashed, looking up at the city floating in the sky.
Magnificent.
Splendid.
Whole to the point of heartlessness.
It hung proudly in the sky, as if it had nothing at all to do with the woman lying cold below.
His hand clenched. Each finger dug deep into his flesh. Blood ran in streams between his fingers and fell. Drop by drop.
But they did not reach the ground.
Before the stunned eyes of those behind him, each drop stopped in midair. Hovering. Then flew back up, clinging to his skin, spreading in patches that covered his body, covering even the coat she had given him. They dried into great plates, from his arms to his chest, bearing the dark hue of blood once it has left the flesh. The streaks of blood were honed smooth into a black iron that reflected the sparks in the sky, clinging to his body like a living curse.
The Apocalypse Warrior once again put on his old shell.
From the wound in his palm, a black fire tore through the flesh, stretching into a razor-sharp blade. The fire burned as fiercely as the very hatred seething within him.
The ash-grey wings beat hard, then shot straight into the snowy clouds.
He did not look back. He knew well that if he allowed this body to linger even a little longer, those sinful wings might no longer have the strength to lift him.
He gripped the hilt tight. The towers behind him slowly sank into darkness beneath the clouds. The fierce wind lashed his angular face. One by one, ash-grey feathers fluttered away from his wings.
In one very unintended moment, a strange drop of liquid gathered at the corner of his left eye.
He did not notice it at all.
Not as clear as water. Nor as dark and thick as blood. It was so clear that the firelight in the sky passed through it easily, keeping only, humbly, a very faint shade of darkness hidden at its heart. The wind quietly pushed it down his scratched cheek, and then it let go of that ice-cold face.
Falling.

Through the grey clouds. Past the twisted roots of The Root of Sin. Falling on and on into a very deep realm, until it silently touched the surface of the lake.
The Discrated Lake.
Drip.
A very small ripple spread out. Not a sound. Not a single ray of light.

Not a single prayer. Not a single echo. Only one brief moment, like a held breath.

One.
Then ten.
A hundred.

Every massacre was cut short by an invisible force, as if a primal instinct that had slept since before they ever took this form had awakened all at once.
The tear of a fallen angel. Something heaven had never once beheld.
No command needed. No forbidden name to call out. They knew well whom they must obey.
Countless burning red dots filled both the sky and the ground. The beating of wings and the trampling of feet thundered across the lower world, like moths rushing toward the flame hanging above the clouds. They clung to the trunk of The Root of Sin, sinking their claws deep, surging upward without thought, turning the very tree of warning into a shortcut leading up to The City of Mercy Dreams. The winged Darksiders surged ahead, close behind the figure of the fallen angel. No covenant. No oath of loyalty. Only the utter submission of instinct.

Drag did not look down. He did not care about whatever was flooding in behind him. He only moved forward. His heart beat heavily in his chest.
Beyond the clouds, the flawless ivory walls of The City of Mercy Dreams grew larger and larger.
Suddenly a long, piercing horn rang down to the mortal world. The colossal gates swung solemnly open. From deep within the sacred realm, a dazzling golden light poured out, tearing through the grey clouds. Rows of solemn figures in white robes appeared.
One.
Ten.
Then hundreds.
The heavenly host descended. Pure white wings filled the whole sky above. In their hands were long, sharp spears, shields inlaid with gleaming gold, bows already drawn. All aimed at the only one bearing a pair of ash-grey wings.
He gazed silently at that majestic sight. His heart beat faster. He drew a deep, cold breath into his swelling chest, then raised two fingers forward.
Tiny beads of blood rose from his blood armor, hovering around him, then rolling into spheres. Hundreds of them, like raindrops trapped when the flow of time no longer moved.
His two fingers cut across the air.
He swung down.
The beads of blood tore through the snow and shot into the ranks of the heavenly host. Shield after shield was pierced. Heavy armor was clawed apart. They kept flying, then changed direction like creatures with minds of their own, pouring all their fury out upon the sky.
Spear after spear.
Pair after pair of white wings.
Falling apart one by one.
He passed through the twisted corpses. Those still standing rushed toward him. A dazzling golden spear swung at him. He only leaned slightly aside, then swung the sword of the apocalypse in a horizontal cut. The body was severed so cleanly that no clang of metal had time to ring out. Only the sound of steel flowing out of the cut.
Another angel charged, driving his spear straight into the black armor. At that very moment, horror appeared on his face. The plates of black iron swallowed the spearhead, as if wanting to drain away every notion of matter that touched it. He let go in panic. But the moment his hand left the shaft, his throat went rigid.
A blood-smeared hand closed around his neck.
And crushed his throat.
He tossed the corpse toward the ranks ahead, then kept charging forward.
This time, thousands of arrows rained down like a storm. The undying armor took them all. The moment the arrowheads touched the dark aura radiating from it, they vanished into nothingness. Nothing could stop him now.
Nothing at all.
Suddenly frenzied roars echoed back up from the ground.
The Darksiders had arrived.
They swarmed in, clinging to the archers, clawing, gnawing apart even the sturdiest shields. Sharp teeth sank into wings. White feathers mixed with red blood stained the whole sky. Fragments of bone. Chunks of flesh. Weapons. Armor. Everything fell in a scatter, as if gravity were trying to drag the things that belonged to the earth back to it.
He did not look. The death of the monsters or the survival of the heavenly host, neither was the blood debt he wanted to collect.
There was someone he needed to find.
He left the battle behind his back and shot straight through the ivory gate.
Inside, the city was so clean that not a single soul was there. Clean enough to chill the back of the neck, compared to everything happening in the lower world. He passed silent libraries, gleaming citadels, pure white corridors without a single trace of blood. No crying. No prayers. As if the end of the world out there had never been worthy of setting foot in this place.
The patterns carved on the buildings slid past his eyes one by one. A familiar feeling slowly crawled down his spine. A memory he did not want to remember at all.
A hand that had once gently touched his wings.
" Will these stains bring you back to this realm? "
" Or will they bring you back to chaos? "
Lifeless fingers brushed across each white feather. No pain. At least not in the first moment. Then each feather began to catch fire. The flames crawled along the wings, burning them away, swallowing them, leaving only a layer of grey ash clinging to the charred bone.
His body lost its balance. The sky turned upside down. The white clouds drifted out of reach.
He fell.
Fell on and on.
Until the sky closed before him one last time.

And on the tower ahead, beside the colossal bell of pure gold, a figure slowly appeared.

Drag stopped. Not out of fear. But because that face unsettled him before he could even understand why.
Hair of piercing white. Not the faded silver of old age, but the white he had seen countless times on himself. On the surface of water. On pools of blood. On the gleaming metal of an enemy's blade. Every line. The shape of the nose. The jaw. Even that arrogant bearing, the way he cast his icy gaze down upon the chaotic world below. A face far too familiar, yet so foreign that it made him uneasy.
Different only in its wholeness.
Clean. Immaculate. Not a single scar. Not a single trace of a body that had once been crushed and then forced to regenerate a thousand times. Nothing at all to prove he had ever had to bear any curse. As if someone above had deliberately looked at Lucifer, then meticulously carved away every part deemed a flaw. The scars. The deviations. The traces of mistakes. Everything he himself had once hated about himself. And yet when they had vanished completely, what remained before his eyes felt more wrong than any wound.

The one on the tower finally raised his face. Red eyes looked straight at him. Calm. Without the slightest guard.
Right over his mouth, a silver hand pressed across. Five lifeless fingers covered his lips entirely, and between the cold gaps, a golden hue glowed dimly.
A Mark.
Like the silver hand that had covered The Eye. Like the silver hand that had embraced The Ear. Only this time, it sealed his mouth shut.
The Mouth of The Speechless Sorrows.
Michael.
The black fire on the blade trembled slightly. Michael still sat unmoving. Not trembling. Not worried. Behind Drag, the sounds of war grew closer and closer. Shattering pieces of buildings, explosions, battle cries, and the echo of chaos itself. But Michael did not bother to glance at it, not even with half an eye. As if it were all only a passing rain, not enough to trouble one who was flawless.

Michael tilted his neck. The five silver fingers over his mouth parted slightly. The gold within lit up. His lips slowly curled.
" So you've come. "
Drag did not answer.
Michael smiled. A flawless smile.
" My brother. "
