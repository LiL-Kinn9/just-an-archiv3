The first wind of autumn carried a chill across a barren citadel in the north of Cvstodia. With it came a stranger, treading heavily toward the massive stone wall, his bare feet still dusted with the sand of the mortal world.
He stopped before the archway. Moss-covered stones were stacked upon one another, rotted through by hundreds of years. Above, dozens of guards held their breath and drew their bowstrings, welcoming the uninvited with arrowheads.
But mortal weapons did not make him falter. He walked straight in, unhurried, like one who had once trodden upon the sky itself.
An iron spear was suddenly thrust across his chest.
" Where is your pass? "
A foreign notion, and seemingly not worth the trouble of understanding. He slowly lowered his gaze to the one who had spoken.
The guard went rigid. A primal terror crawled up his spine. Or rather, what crushed his lungs were the blazing red eyes boring straight into his soul. The guard's gaze darted down the stranger's body, then flinched away as if burned, and he took it for fear of the proper kind.
The spear trembled as it was withdrawn.
" W... Welcome to the city, sir. "
He did not bother to part his lips, and strode silently through the darkness beneath the archway.
Beyond the walls lay a filthy market stretching through narrow streets. Merchants from across the continent crowded together, fighting over every inch of ground, trading provisions, fruit, livestock and lifeless metals. In his eyes, it was all worthless dregs.
The deeper he went, the thicker the herd of people grew, and nausea struck straight at his senses: the sour stench of sweat, the foul reek of mud, and the rank smell of lambs lying on the butcher's block, waiting to die. Perhaps this realm had never been meant for him.
Prying eyes began to turn toward the stranger. Some glanced and hastily looked away, others leaned close to whisper. He took it for the meekness of sheep before a predator, and it only deepened his disgust. At the very least, he told himself he did not care.
His steps quickened without his noticing, until his attention struck a colossal structure: a stone tower driven straight into the heart of the market. It had likely stood there since he passed the gate; he had simply never looked. On the crumbling bricks at its base, a line of words had been crudely chiseled.
" Manlanier, The City of Lambs. "
A city of sheep. He had barely curled his lip, wondering why mankind would give the place they lived such a pitiful name, when a dazed figure slammed into him.
The person collapsed onto the road. A basket flew from their hands, spilling bright red chili peppers that rolled across the grey stones.
Yet another nuisance had come looking for him. He looked down: a small frame nearly swallowed by a dark fur coat, the hood pulled low, head bowed as if deliberately refusing to look up. The person hurried to their knees to gather what had fallen, wholly ignoring the pitch-black shadow standing right over them.
Carrying an arrogance without equal, he merely folded his arms and watched. He wanted to see what this fragile creature would do when it raised its face.
" It seems you cannot see me? "
Not a word in reply. Not even a wary glance. The silence tore through his pride like a blade.
Having gathered everything, the person stood up and brushed past him indifferently, as though he were nothing but a stone statue. He froze. No creature in this world had ever dared to ignore him so.
" You dare ignore my words like that? "
The cloaked figure stopped short. At last, the moment he had been waiting for. He had already pictured a pale face, a trembling that mortals ought to have. But he had never guessed who stood before him, until a weightless voice rose from beneath the hood.
" You do know you're not wearing anything, right? "
He was dumbstruck.
" Huh? "
His eyes instinctively dropped, sweeping over the completely naked lower half of his body, then rose to look at her again. His arrogant mind could not yet grasp the meaning of the question, only dimly realizing that the glances and whispers along the road might not have been entirely because of his eyes.
A sigh, plain as day, escaped the woman. She raised two fingers and beckoned lightly toward herself, as if ordering him to follow.
He stood rooted to the spot. He could not see why one from the heavens should obey a mortal stranger. Perhaps because he could not smell fear on her, an anomaly unlike anything since he had fallen to the mortal world.
She turned her back and walked off, not bothering to look back to see whether he followed. A confidence bordering on arrogance. And yet his bare feet quietly followed in her steps.
He kept just enough distance, carrying the suspicion of some hidden scheme. Or at least, he told himself that was why he could not take his eyes off her every step. Her haughty bearing gradually drowned out the noise and stench of the market, and then he realized this woman carried a very pleasant scent. He searched for every reason to deny such a sentimental thought, in vain. His mind held nothing but questions now: why was she not afraid of him? Why help him? And why was he still following this back?
After a few turns, she led him into a small, forlorn tailor's shop at the end of the street. He lingered to let her cross the threshold first, a gesture that seemed courteous, or merely let his instincts feel safer before unfamiliar things.
The smell of cloth and dust struck his nose the moment the door opened. He narrowed his eyes, sweeping over rotten wooden shelves and a few strips of fabric bearing crude stitching. While he was still assessing the room, she had come close and held a dark fur coat against him to measure it. His feet instinctively stepped back a beat.
" This doesn't seem to suit someone like you, does it? "
Her gaze slid slowly down, stopping at his lower half.
" I think you'd better put on some trousers first. "
He followed where her eyes had landed.
" Is something wrong? "
She was silent for a moment, then let out a soft laugh.
" It doesn't look very long, does it? "
He frowned, his pride stirring uncomfortably.
" What is long? "
She answered lightly.
" The sword. "
He looked about in bewilderment, left and then right. There was not a single blade to be found in this cramped room. Right then, a pair of leather trousers flew straight into his face.
" Never mind, it's nothing. Put these on. "
He ripped the fabric from his face and shoved his arms into the legs as though putting on a shirt. Stopping short, he stared at the tangled thing in his hands.
" What the hell? What am I supposed to do? "
No answer. He looked up only to find that the woman had left the room at some point, leaving him alone with the stupid leather trousers. A somewhat humiliating confrontation. After a long struggle, he finally forced the coarse fabric into its proper place, then threw the fur coat over his shoulders.
Just then, the shuffle of footsteps sounded from the front room.
" The coat and trousers are four Chromee, miss. "
It was not her voice. He hurried out, just in time to see the outermost wooden door swing shut. She was gone, faster than he could realize.
Behind the counter was only an aged tailor. Four rough silver coins lay on the wooden counter. The old man's hands shook as he made the sign of the cross, his terrified eyes never leaving him. That look again. He could not be bothered to care any further, and strode out of the shop.
A cold wind swept through the trees along the street, as if urging them to make way for a withering autumn. Layers of dead leaves already lay flat along both edges of the road. He stopped for a moment. The wind seemed to carry something from very far away, a feeling he could not remember how many eras had passed since he last knew. The place where he once reigned had never known such decay, only bottomless expanses and eternal white clouds: no seasons, no falling leaves, nothing that slowly faded and let go of its branch.
He looked at the dry leaves beneath his feet. It seemed this lower world was more pleasant than he had thought. Or perhaps it was the woman from before who had made it less wretched. He loathed such a soft thought.
Since falling from heaven, everyone who laid eyes on him had either panicked or found a way to avoid him. Only she had not. No fear, no caution, she was even calm enough to leave him behind with a pair of leather trousers. If only he knew her name. He fooled himself into believing all he wanted was a name.
He glanced around the bustling market. Among the crowding shadows, the hem of the dark fur coat flickered again in the distance. He recognized her at once, and his legs had lunged forward before he could even think.
Only a few strides, and doubt rose to block his way. Why, of all things? She was only a mortal he had barely met. Just for a name? How foolish. Then he suddenly remembered the cloth he now wore. Four Chromee. Yes, he needed to return the four silver coins she had spent on a stranger.
He searched the leather trousers. Not a single pocket, not a single coin. The excuse he had just built had half collapsed. But it was still enough to keep his ego from questioning him for a few more steps.
The crowd hid her from view again. Only moments ago he had been disgusted at having to rub shoulders with these people, and now he was the one pushing bodies aside, until the hem of that coat came fully into view.
She was kneeling beside a forlorn stall. Before her was a pot of white flowers. Nothing precious: a few scrawny stems, a little black soil, petals so thin that even the first wind of autumn was enough to make them tremble. And yet she lifted one stem with both hands, slowly, her fingertips gently brushing across the petals.
He stopped. The four Chromee suddenly did not seem so urgent.
He did not understand. A thing that knew no pain, no fear, that could not even beg her not to let go, what was there in it worth cherishing?
The wind sent a few dead leaves skittering along the road, and one caught on his shoulder. He still did not move. He had stood watching too long, and strangely, he wanted to stand a little longer.
She turned slightly. He immediately stepped forward, as if the movement could hide that he had been standing there watching. His hand touched her shoulder lightly.
She turned around. The hood was blown from her head by the wind, letting her jet-black hair fall around cheeks flushed red from the cold. Before he could take in the rest of her features, a pale golden hue had already seized his eyes.
Her left eye. The color of dusk before the sun consents to leave the horizon, the color of the leaves fading around Manlanier.
His body stiffened. Not fear. He knew what fear was, or at least he thought he knew. This was older than that, like a wound long since healed suddenly aching again before he could even recall ever being wounded. He tried to dig through the depths of his mind. Empty.
" Do you need anything else? "
Her voice pulled him out of that withered place. He was silent for a beat.
" What is your name? "
She did not answer right away. She only reached toward him in silence. His body leaned back slightly on reflex, but those fingers were faster and touched his shoulder. She brushed off the dead leaf caught there. It spun a few times in the wind, then vanished into the crowd.
He watched her hand withdraw. A touch so light it should not have been worth remembering. Unfortunately, he remembered it.
The wind gusted across the street, sending dry leaves whirling around them both. He had once reigned in the highest place, a place mankind could not reach even if they bowed their heads all their lives, yet nothing in heaven had ever made him want to linger a little longer. And now, he stood rooted before a mortal whose name he did not yet know, in a city he had been cursing only moments ago, in an autumn he did not yet know how deeply he would remember.
He did not understand why. Perhaps, for now, there was no need to understand. The pale gold was still there, and this time he truly had more time to admire it.
His most beautiful autumn.
