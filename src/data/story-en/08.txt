" Krisanta. "
A deep, warm voice rang out from the towering sky. Amid the murky clouds, the presence of a colossal heart covered the whole vault of heaven.
" Take this eye. "
" Bel'vi. Keep this ear. "
Bright red blood flowed down from heart valves cast in gold.
" The last Mark. Michael. Take hold of it. "
Streak after streak of blood dripped down, pouring over the heads of the three chosen.
Kristin opened her eyes. Raindrops rolled down her pale gold pupil, and The Eye of The Ignored Sorrows had fully awakened. In that instant, the sword in Drag's hand melted into a pool of liquid gold, and he missed the beat of his thrust.
A stroke of the sword, thin as a thread of gold, grazed across his neck.
---
Everything before his eyes blurred. A feeling of floating. He remembered this feeling, that emptiness eating into the depths of his memory, as if something light and white had left him and fallen forever without ever touching anything. The raindrops hung motionless in midair. His sight turned upside down, and he saw his own body still standing before her. A headless corpse.
Once again. This rotten fate. This emptiness, so familiar that he no longer found it strange.
" When the Apocalypse Warrior appears, you are charged with stopping him. "
That voice still echoed in the air. Perhaps everything still had to unfold according to that one's will. He only wished her warmth had not been arranged, and the moment he wished it, he already knew it would never be answered.
It began from a place very small and very real, from the wound she had left that night that he had not yet understood. Then it grew, and he could no longer tell what was his own pain and what was something pushing from behind, driving that pain beyond the limits he knew of himself. He was not sure whether it was his. He only knew it was carrying him away.
A red spread across both his eyes.
---
Kristin bent far back just as the drop of blood from the headless corpse's finger tore through the wind toward her. She kicked its chest, flinging it far away, while the white carnation buds, remnants of the flower sword, pushed up and pierced his flesh from the inside out. Red flesh fell in flakes onto the broken bricks. But the wounds closed, and the corpse staggered to its feet, its hand forming the sign again.
She beat her wings and shot up through the crack in the dome. The second shot only grazed her cheek, leaving a thin scratch, then crashed into the bronze bell atop the tower. The whole tower shook. The bell's roof collapsed, a warped, torn clang of bronze echoing across the grey sky.
She looked down. The corpse bent over, grabbed a fistful of its own white hair, lifted its head from the bricks and threw it straight at her. Midway through its flight, vertebra after vertebra stretched from the cut, strands of flesh wove together, and a new body wrapped around the skeleton. He was reborn right in midair, his ash-grey wings bursting open, and shot upward.
She dove down to meet him. The tip of the golden sword aimed straight at the heaving left side of his chest, and he did not dodge. He did not raise a hand to block, did not change course, only held an absolute straight line of destruction.
The tip of the sword plunged into the left of his chest.
" Clang. "
She had heard that sound once, in the darkness of last night, when her hand rested on his chest. A harsh metallic ring, as if something very ancient and indestructible were crushing the holy blade. But this time, at a distance of only a breath, she looked straight into his eyes and realized something more terrifying than the sound. Nothing answered back. The red eyes were hollow, like an evil spirit that existed only to destroy. The Dragalon she had known seemed never to have stood in this body.
The Eye flushed with a warning glow. She folded herself down. From his bloodied palm, a black sword sprang out, black fire licking the whole blade, a fire the color of resentment and grief. She kicked away, and in that leap swung her hand in two consecutive slashes. His ash-grey wings were cut clean off. Tens of thousands of blood-soaked black feathers burst out, darkening a whole stretch of sky.
He fell into the abyss without a groan, only two red dots slowly sinking behind the clouds.
Kristin did not give chase. She beat her wings higher, into the midst of the churning black clouds, until the ground was erased from her sight and the world held only the wind whistling in her ears and her heart pounding in her chest. She took a deep breath, telling herself these clouds would hide her from his hunting gaze.
A sound tore through the wind.
A colossal streak of black fire split the clouds, slashing straight down from above as if to cleave the sky in two. She leaned aside to dodge, but not fast enough. The blade swept past and severed one of her white wings. Fresh blood sprayed from the root of the wing, mixing into the cold rain. Her body lurched, her wingbeats faltering. Six majestic pairs of wings, now only eleven.
The Eye still glowed, but it had been completely blind to that slash. It had caught no flicker of killing intent, no wicked scheme appeared before the blade came down. It had simply happened.
Unease stretched taut as a string through every strand of her flesh. The golden eye darted again and again, sweeping through every layer of cloud, searching.
Then she saw him. He hung upside down halfway up the sky, head pointed at the ground. Two bony fingers rose, and she leaned away on reflex, but nothing came at her. The two fingers only crooked lightly toward each other. Not aimed at her.
Something was wrong.
She whipped around. The streak of black fire from before had turned back, rushing toward her back, only a finger's breadth away. She bent herself double, and the black streak grazed past, searing right before her eyes, then vanished into the clouds.
She turned back to where she had been, and the upside-down shadow was gone. She looked up, and he was right above her head, blotting out the whole sky.
It was no longer Drag. His face was imprisoned behind an iron helm, two demonic horns curving down to hug his jawbone. The armor was blacker than the deepest darkness in the human self, the breath of death seeping from every seam of metal. His eyes blazed like hellfire, glaring straight through her.
Kristin heard no breath. No clash of armor, not a single living sound came from that body. The absolute silence chilled the back of her neck. Not a single threat, not a single roar. Only the pitch-black sword of the apocalypse, raised high at some point, waiting to strike down.
She gritted her teeth and gripped the hilt tight. The voice of The High Wills echoed back in her mind.
" You are charged with stopping him. "
The shape in the prophecy stood right before her eyes in flesh and blood.
---
He looked at her through the slit of his helm, as if looking through a very distant window. He saw his arm raised high, saw the black sword, but could not feel their weight. The rage had risen to his throat. Beneath it, other things were dying out: the warmth of a back he had once carried along the corridors, the sweetness of the apple, the fragile weight of her hand in his. One by one they went cold, as if someone were pulling them out of him. He was losing the things that made him himself, with nothing left to hold on and tell them apart.
Then something white glided past the slit.
A petal. It was a remnant of the flower sword she had raised from the very carnation petals, swept up from the wound in his chest, turning slowly in the rain. It bore exactly the white of her wings, and fell slowly like a feather from very far back in his memory.
He remembered. In the middle of the shabby market, she had knelt beside the pot of white flowers, lifting a stem with her fingertips, so gently, as if afraid the first wind of autumn would make it fall. He remembered how she brushed the dead leaf from his shoulder and pretended nothing had happened. He remembered how she tilted her shoulder to the left whenever she was thinking about something she would not say, and her weight on his back, lighter than anything he had ever carried. No one had taught her those small gestures. No prophecy could write them.
The blinding gold before him was The Eye, was the sword, was the mission He had placed in her. But behind it, he believed, that woman was still there. Just a little, that person was still there.
He did not know whether it was his own idea or only something he chose to believe. But he chose it.
The sword of the apocalypse came down.
He threw all his strength into an arm that was no longer his own, not to strike down but to pull it off course. The slash veered a hand's span, a small gap between the blade and her shoulder, and the black fire swept through the empty space beside her, cleaving an empty stretch of cloud in two.
Within that very slash, the black armor began to crack. Piece after piece of metal shattered, peeled away, turned to ash, and was swept from his body by the wild wind. The black sword in his hand dissolved into nothingness. The ash-grey wings fell apart into tens of thousands of feathers. The red in his eyes faded, softened, then went out.
He let go. He did not spread his wings. He fell like a stone into the abyss of the mortal world.
---
Kristin stood still amid the grey clouds. The ash of the armor drifted past her face, carrying the smell of a fire gone cold. She waited for a pain that never came, and realized she was still whole. That slash, too, had carried not a flicker of ill intent for The Eye to see in time. She did not know whether it was luck or someone's effort, and she did not yet want to name it.
She looked down at the falling figure. Just before the fog swallowed him, she saw those rough lips move slightly. No sound rose, but with a strange understanding, she read the last word he tried to say before letting go.
" Kristin? "
