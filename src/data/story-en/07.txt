The rain began to fall along with the streak of sunlight. Each freezing drop pierced through the broken panes, pouring onto the ruined bricks, slowly mixing into and then swallowing the bright red pools of blood spreading there.
" Lucifer. "
The name still hung in the great hall of Mother of Mothers. A name he had thought dead and rotted along with the fall of the sky. And yet with a single whisper, Kristin had dug it back into the world of the living.
Drag braced a hand on the stone floor and forced himself up. His tongue had grown back at some point, but the sour, shrewish taste still refused to leave his mouth. The golden sword was still driven through the left side of his chest, right where his heart had just lost its rhythm beneath her hand. Kristin did not pull the sword out. He did not bother to move either. What tore at him now was no longer the blade, but countless questions pouring into his mind until he could not breathe.
That name.
" What did you just call me? "
" Lucifer. "
This time it could not be a trick of his ears. Could not be something he deliberately misheard.
-Lucifer-
He frowned. A voice from some faraway realm suddenly echoed back in his mind. Not Kristin's voice. Deep, warm, gentle enough to chill the back of the neck, and it was that false compassion that made his throat churn with disgust.
" The First Child of The Miracle. "
A blinding white void flashed through the broken fragments of memory. A hand made of gold. Lifeless, cold fingers pressed against his brow, and a dazzling light peeled him away from reality. He blinked. The memory burst like foam. Before him there was only that woman.
" How do you know that name? "
She did not answer. Instead, her hand slowly bore down on the hilt. Strand after strand of flesh was split by the blade, blood flooding onto the bricks. The severed bundles of muscle around the blade convulsed violently, the instinct of his body trying to push the foreign object out. Drag did not take his eyes off her face. Kristin quietly watched it all.
" So it really is you. "
He did not resist. Not because his strength had run out. He only needed an answer.
" Who told you? "
Kristin stepped back half a pace.
" No one. "
" I hate lies. "
" So do I, Dragalon. "
She hesitated.
" Ah... Lucifer. "
A choking weight crawled down his spine. Two names, set side by side like a mockery of fate, and worse, spoken from those lips. Dragalon. The name she called when she pulled him by the hand through the streets of the lower world. When she made him swallow bland food. When she laughed without a care. When she curled up and fell asleep in his arms. Lucifer. A name that had never once existed in those peaceful dreams.
Kristin let go of the hilt. Rain poured through the broken dome, falling on the golden hilt still drenched in his blood, slowly diluting the red.
" I wanted to be sure. "
She raised a finger still stained with blood and touched it lightly to her lips.
" Sure of what? "
" That you truly cannot die. "
Her voice dropped on the last word.
The hall sank into silence. Drag looked down at the wound in his chest. The shattered strands of flesh had already begun to crawl, closing around the blade.
" You did all this just to know that? "
" No. "
She was silent for a beat.
" I saw the color of those wings. Your blood.. "
Drag looked down at the sword in front of his chest. Then up at the coarse strip of cloth he had once clumsily wrapped around her shoulder. A cut too straight, too clean. Surely the same thing.
" Your wound.. you made it yourself. "
Kristin did not deny it.
" I needed to know what you would do when I bled. "
He had come. Running through the fog, through the cornfield, through a monster that had just severed his arm. He had knelt beside her, holding her in his arms as if she were the only thing in this world that could break.
" And you came. "
Her voice held not a trace of triumph. The corners of her lips pressed together, then let go.
Only now did he understand everything that had happened. It was a pity it was far too late.
Kristin lowered her eyes to The Miracle of Pains in her hands. The book had been at his back since last night. He did not know when she had taken it.
" I saw this when I was little. Now I see it again, grown up. "
She let her gaze linger on his face one breath longer.
" Only now do I realize it speaks of you more than I ever remembered. "
He turned his face and spat out the clot of blood still lodged in his throat. Only a few beats ago, he had let that hand touch his rotten heart, let her listen to every sob of it, let her see with her own eyes a weakness that even he had never dared admit. He had thought that naked surrender was the very end of understanding. But before Kristin's resolute gaze, even that was not enough.
She turned on her heel, treading on the broken glass, heading toward the great stone statue.
" Do you know why they built Mother of Mothers? They say that a very long time ago, when Cvstodia was still a land of divided territories, a supreme being appeared. "
Drag did not watch her back.
" They called Him The High Wills. "
His fingers twitched slightly. Only a tiny reflex beneath the skin, but Kristin had caught it. She always saw through too many things.
" He gave mankind their harvests. Gave them light. Gave them laws. And in return... "
Kristin glanced at the rotting confession pews along both sides of the hall.
" Mankind had to kneel. "
Drag burst out laughing. A grating, broken sound, without half a measure of joy in it.
" The story they told you? "
Kristin turned around.
" Then what do you know? "
Drag looked up, glaring at the colossal stone statue. No longer the vague unease of when he first walked in. At this moment he knew for certain, he hated the lifeless stone being carved into that sacred form before him.
" I know him. "
Kristin froze.
" Him? "
Drag moved his gaze and looked straight at her.
" The thing you all worship. "
A breathless silence. Outside, the rain grew heavier and heavier. Water seeped through the cracks in the stone, gathering into small, winding streams across the hall floor, carrying the blood of them both. The red slowly paled. Kristin lowered her eyes to follow the ragged streams.
" That book had a few rather interesting lines. "
The unholy book he had once wished she had never laid a hand on. Kristin half-closed her eyes and began to read. She did not look at the page. Each line flowed out, even, without stress, like a mold releasing letter by letter words that had taken root in her ten thousand lifetimes ago.
" The day betrayal covers all the lower world. A miracle is born. Punishment descends upon all of Cvstodia. Every sinful soul cannot leave its flesh. "
Drag fell silent. An old, rotten feeling crawled back into his mind. Like standing at the edge of an abyss, instinct knowing full well that beneath the fog lay something terrible, while his heart was too cowardly to look down.
" Mankind has no right to die. Still less the right to live. A black knight with ash-grey wings. "
Kristin opened her eyes. A gaze sharp as a razor pinned onto him.
" With a heart torn into two halves. One half stays. One half leaves. In his hand a sword burning with resentment. He shall end the chaos. "
Her voice stayed level, neither rising nor falling.
" The world calls him the Apocalypse Warrior. "
The rain still howled. Drag stood still as a statue. Not because he feared that garbage of a prophecy. In his eyes, those rotting pages were no different from a ready-made mold built to force living souls into it. But every line, every word spoken was exactly like an invisible hand crushing him and stuffing him into that mold, before he had even been given the right to choose himself.
Kristin tilted her head.
" So what should I call you? "
A bitter smile flickered on his lips.
" A stupid story. "
" Are you afraid? "
The smile died out.
" Do not test me. "
Kristin looked down at the golden sword still pinned in his chest, then looked up.
" I don't think you're afraid of the prophecy. I think you're afraid of remembering it. "
The words drove straight into the dark corner he had always denied. And that was the spark. Not because she called him Lucifer. Nor because the sword was still piercing his chest. But because this small woman had stepped too close to a truth that he himself was still basely running from.
Blood trickled along the edge of the blade.
Drip. Drip.
Drag gripped the hilt tight. Kristin did not stop him; she only stepped back half a pace. He gritted his teeth and yanked hard. The blade tore free of his chest, blood splattering onto his chin, his neck, clinging to every rough finger. The gaping wound convulsed, slimy strands of flesh crisscrossing, frantically closing. Drag looked at the sword in his hand. That weightless metal fit his hand so well it was unpleasant.
Ahead, she still did not waver. No smile. No idle teasing. As if she had never been the woman who had just whined for him to carry her along the ruined corridors.
At least.
That was the thin lie he was forcing himself to believe. Because if the soul within that body was still the woman for whom he was willing to throw away his arrogance, then his mind would fall apart until he choked.
The rain soaked the strip of cloth he had wrapped around her shoulder, as if to wash away the little tenderness he had once given.
The blood flowing over his body suddenly stopped falling. Each drop hung in midair. One. Two. Then all of them flew back up, coiling around his two fingertips, compressing into a heavy red dot. He raised his hand.
Fire.
Kristin leaned aside to dodge. The streak of blood grazed her thin shoulder, then slammed straight into the colossal statue behind her.
BOOM.
A great chunk of stone on the statue burst apart. The dome shook, tiles and rubble crashing down. Kristin's figure vanished behind a curtain of rain and dust. He slowly lowered his hand. A hollow suddenly opened in his chest. He did not wait to see whether she had been crushed. He knew he never wanted to face that answer. Only, he still did not know what he truly wanted, if not her death.
Within the grey veil of dust, a mass of matter slowly took shape. Enormous and grotesque. A misshapen cocoon sealed tight, lying there in absolute silence. Drag gripped the golden hilt tight, rubble crunching around his bare feet. A thin crack scratched across the surface of the cocoon. Then another. Dozens of fractures crawled across it like a spider's web, and a tearing sound split the air throughout the hall. The shell burst open, and a gale swept away the thick dust.
Six pairs of pure white wings spread wide all at once.
Pure to the point of being overwhelming. Radiant and blinding. A beauty so holy it was stifling, making the dark instinct within him seethe at once with the will to fight. But beneath that seething was something else, smaller and sharper: he had once borne that pure white. He remembered the wind of heaven slipping through every feather, remembered a sky that never knew autumn. He remembered, and then he hated the very thing he remembered.
White feathers drifted through the curtain of rain and dust. Kristin stood at the center of that splendor, slowly descending from the light. The moment her bare feet touched the filthy stone floor, a pure white carnation instantly burst into bloom. Then another cluster. The roots of the flowers crept through every crack, covering the rubble, spreading throughout the hall. Blood from the wound on her shoulder slowly dripped down, gently touching each white petal.
Kristin. Still that delicate, haughty face. Still the lips he had just drowned in. Still the form that had curled up asleep in his arms. But something had slammed shut behind the depths of those eyes. An invisible door. And he no longer knew which side of it he stood on.
Kristin's left hand slowly lowered. Petals gathered out of the empty air, white as the twelve wings behind her, then stretched, smoothed, and gleamed with exactly the same gold as the weapon lying in his hand. Kristin grasped the hilt and pointed the tip of the sword toward him.
Drag cast aside all hesitation. He lunged, a stroke of the sword splitting the air. Kristin turned, the blade only grazing the edge of her shirt. He twisted his wrist sharply and swung a horizontal slash. She arched upward and flew, weightless, as if gravity had been stripped of the right to hold her. Her pale gold left eye suddenly began to glow. Just as his sword swept through the empty air, she landed a kick straight into his shoulder. His body flipped and was flung back.
An unnatural, piercing pain appeared right in the flesh she had just touched. He looked over. Something was pushing up through his skin. A bud of piercing white. Then a second, a third. Pure carnation buds took root right in his flesh and blood, their roots twining tight, piercing through every strand of flesh, drinking his blood greedily like one who had died of thirst a thousand years.
Drag clenched his jaw. He did not give them one more second to bloom.
The golden sword slashed down. Half his shoulder and his arm were severed, falling with a thud onto the broken stone. The white buds still swayed, showing off their color on the flesh that had just been cut away. Blood from the cut gushed like a waterfall, then stopped dead, and a whole arm grew back in the blink of an eye.
Kristin quietly watched the process. A very faint smile appeared on her lips. He did not know whether it was mockery or relief. But he had caught it with his eyes. Once again, he had to endure seeing an expression all too familiar on the face of one trying to become a stranger. The irony only dug deeper into the fury in his chest.
Drag roared and shot forward. A straight thrust of the sword, aimed at the blazing golden eye.
Kristin did not dodge. Did not retreat even half a step. She only stood there, seeing through him. The gold in her pupil flared. A thin, razor-sharp ring of light began to spin around her pupil.
Drag stopped dead. Not because his reason had ordered it.
The sword in his hand shook violently. The metal began to melt, drops of red-hot pure gold falling, searing his palm. At the same moment, the hand she held her sword with also trembled for one beat. He did not know whether what was melting was her magic, or her resolve. But his mind no longer had room to care about the sword.
Drag stared fixedly into that eye. A pale, haunting gold. The color of dusk he had come face to face with on the very first day he set foot in the Manlanier market. The thing that had sown unease into his veins before he even knew her name. A deeply familiar feeling. No. It went far beyond a mere feeling. It was a sealed fragment of memory: an eternal blinding white sky, small shadows bowing to their knees below, a commanding voice crashing down from the endless summit.
" Behold. "
Drag's eyes widened. A name broke through the frozen seal, rising from the deepest place of his memory.
" The Eye... "
His voice was low and hoarse, shattered. Kristin still stood there, judging him with that divine light. At last he understood why, from their very first meeting, her left eye had made his instinct want to look away. Not because he recognized Kristin the person. But because the power asleep within him had sniffed out the divine being dwelling inside her body.
" The Eye of The Ignored Sorrows. "
