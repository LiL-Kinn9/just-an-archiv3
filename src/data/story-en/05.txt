The bell rang long, echoing through the thick fog over the city.
A figure descended softly from the silent sky. Bare feet touched the cold roof tiles, the ash-grey wings slowly folding, shedding drops of night dew that pattered onto the bricks below. His arms still held the woman close. She had sunk into a deeper sleep than he had thought.
He looked down into the night. This place seemed safer than the freezing blue mist howling out there. He leapt down, crumbling stone steps falling away behind him, until his bare feet trod on dry, barren gravel.
Before him was a line of words carved deep into an ancient stone slab, moss creeping and taking root in every crack of time.
" Mother of Mothers. "
He did not understand what the name meant. He only knew this place gave off a smell that made him deeply uneasy, and the cause stood right before the colossal gate.
A cross.
He had never felt at ease looking at one. That shape seemed to claw open a wound that could never close. But at least these ruins were the best choice for the situation at hand. For Kristin.
He carried her inside. Pallid moonlight slipped through the cracks in the rotting walls, lighting every mote of dust suspended in the air. Mankind had abandoned this place a very long time ago. Damp, moldy pews stood in dark rows, and on the altar a few burnt-out candles were nothing but long drips of wax. He swept his arm and cleared away everything in his sight. The holy chalices clattered to the floor, the dry sound echoing through the hall. The sacred had to give way to a human body.
Drag gently laid her down on the bulky table, tore off a long strip of cloth draped across the altar, then clumsily bound the bleeding wound on her shoulder. Layer upon layer. Then layer upon layer again. The red seeping through the cloth left his mind restless. That liquid had the color of his own blood, the same rank smell, yet its nature was entirely different. He was a creation cast away, while she was a living soul held in the palm of a god. She carried within her something he could not reach even if he traded thousands of years.
His scattered thoughts threw off his focus. He unconsciously pulled the bandage tighter than necessary.
" Ahh.. "
Her strained moan was faint, but enough to crush his every movement.
" You will die if you keep on like this. "
After a while of fumbling, the wound finally stopped seeping. He quietly sat down right beside her, looking down at his hands still filthy with streaks of dried blood. He should leave this unholy place. And he should leave her, too. Too many nuisances had blocked his way tonight, and he did not want to be dragged into the troubles of the mortal world. Or, deep inside, he did not want her life to be dragged into his stains.
The stone statue reigning at the end of the hall only strengthened that thought. A sculpture carved in the image of the one he had always despised, the figure to whom all of mankind bowed and offered themselves, though that shape was wholly warped from what he had once beheld. A kind smile? A compassionate gaze? Only shamelessness came to his mind now. How could those people cling so blindly to such a shallow form?
" Ignorant scum. "
He made up his mind to leave. Only his legs stayed rigid, rooted in a feeling he could not name. Weariness? He had never known weariness, with this immortal blood surging through his flesh. Aimlessness? Yes. After leaving this place, he did not know where the roads would take him either.
The very moment he rose, an incredibly weak pull clung to the hem of his dark coat. He lowered his gaze. Those small, drained fingers again. Not a single syllable left her lips, only a soft gaze stretched across the darkness. Deep in her pale eyes, he read her naked longing: to have him stay, to linger beside her a little longer. Or perhaps, it was his own longing.
Drag quietly sat back down and drew her hand into his cold palm. He truly wanted to understand why such fragile flesh could bring a warmth greater than any garment. His rough fingers carefully traced each thin knuckle. That strange feeling again.
Pleasant.
He stayed silent for a while. Then lingered a while longer. With no wish at all to let go.
After so many dim moons, so many decades rotting in the mortal world, he suddenly recalled his meaningless days of wandering. Looking for something? He had no answer. He wanted to be honest with her that even he did not know. But some feeble sounds were best buried forever in the throat. He gazed at the fragile soul drifting asleep before him, a little longer, then longer still, until the first ray of sunlight slipped through the broken door.
Her faint voice pulled him up from the depths of his thoughts.
" Don't you need to sleep? "
" I do not. "
Kristin struggled to sit up, her delicate face wincing in pain.
" Why? "
" No need. "
She glanced down at the coarse cloth wrapped around her shoulder for a moment, then slowly moved her gaze to him.
" Did you do this? It looks so ugly. "
He was stuck for words, his brows crashing together.
" Then why did you not sit up and do it yourself? "
Her clear laughter suddenly burst out, extinguishing the stifling silence of the room.
" Then it's pretty. "
He turned his gaze elsewhere, refusing to believe the teasing.
Kristin wearily leaned back against the damp, cold corner, then searched in her skirt pocket and pulled out a slightly bruised red apple. She wiped it on the hem of her shirt.
" Have you had breakfast? "
" Breakfast? Why should I? "
" Then do it now. "
The apple was pushed into his palm before he could protest. He stared at it, bewildered. Round, cool, its skin still holding warmth from her pocket.
Her eyes wandered around the hall, then rose to the enormous dome overhead, where the elaborate, bizarre patterns gradually grew clear in the morning light.
" I think I've been here before. The church of Mother of Mothers. My mother used to bring me here on weekends. "
He calmly propped his chin on his hand, quietly fixing his eyes on each of her features.
" So how long since you last came? "
" Quite a while, a few years or more. "
Her fingertip lightly traced the thick layer of dust on the stone table.
" It was probably abandoned after the purge of the priests. "
A sardonic half-smile tugged at the corner of Drag's lips.
" Of course. "
Kristin turned her face fully toward him.
" What? Don't you like this place? "
He did not want to dig for an answer to that question. The rotting relics in this room only revived a few pathetic fragments of memory he had worked hard to bury. He turned the apple in his hand, looking at it instead of her.
" This place reeks of those who only know how to kneel. "
Kristin calmly pointed straight at the stone statue at the end of the hall.
" Or is it that you don't like him? "
The image of the one he loathed again, the one he did not want to mention, even as a remnant in his thoughts.
" You ask a lot, do you not? "
She raised her brows and fired back without hesitation.
" Answers have a hard time leaving your mouth, don't they? "
" Yes. "
" Yes what? "
" I do not like him. "
She fell quiet, her gaze turning toward the moldy pews, as if she no longer wanted to hear more curt answers.
" When I was little, my mother often brought me here to pray. But I only wanted to sit by the flower bed behind the church. I liked that spot. "
Drag's gaze dropped, passing over the coarse bandage wrapped across her thin shoulder. He stood up decisively.
" I will go see it. "
A perfect excuse to avoid her interrogation. Because the image of her kneeling to cherish a white flower stem in that shabby market suddenly crept back into his mind. He only wanted to know what was in the flower bed behind the church that was worth so much of her thought.
Kristin frowned, her displeasure plain.
" And what about me? "
" You stay put. "
" No, there's no reason I should listen to you. "
She stubbornly strained to stand, but the unclosed wound immediately punished her weak body. He could not understand why this small person was so ridiculously stubborn.
" You are truly foolish. Why not stay put for a moment? "
" Then why don't you carry me on your back? "
He let out a long sigh. Mankind truly was a bothersome species beyond explanation. Had it been anyone else, those legs would not still be in one piece. Even more ironic, he realized he truly wanted to try carrying this headstrong woman. If her body was warmer than the coat he wore, there was no reason to refuse. At least, that was the only reason that could convince his great ego.
He tucked the apple into the inner fold of his coat, where the four Chromee lay, then silently turned his back and lowered his broad shoulders.
" Then get on, quickly. "
Kristin's thin arms slowly wrapped around his neck. He felt every inch of her skin brush past the nape of his neck, then she shifted her weight and climbed fully onto his sturdy back. Her body was lighter than anything he had imagined.
The two of them left the darkness of the hall and turned into a long, deep corridor. Stained, broken panes of colored glass echoed a little of what remained of the waning moon, casting ragged shadows on the walls. In the glass that remained were three sheep writhing over one another beneath a faded halo. The first face had only an old patch of color covering its eyes. The second was covered by dark bands over its ears. The mouth of the last had long since broken out of the pane. Drag only glanced at them. Another religious motif. He did not bother to stop.
Kristin gazed absently after the patches of mottled color.
" My mother used to carry me on her back like this too. She'd tell me old stories. "
" Such as? "
His bare-footed stride slowed without his noticing.
" Such as the legends of the north. The one I remember most is Noah's Dream. They say that in faraway days, as more and more people appeared, their sins also grew greater, and vows could no longer stop the end of the world. So a rain fell across the whole continent for forty moons, washing away everything still alive on the earth. "
Her arms tightened gently around his neck, her cheek pressed close to the ear of the one carrying her.
" Except for one man and what was on his ship. "
" Who? "
He blurted out the question, unable to hide his curiosity. Kristin giggled softly, as if to savor this rare look of foolishness.
" Noah, The Chosen One. He used a whole forest just to build an enormous ship, and invited every beast aboard, one pair of each kind, male and female. "
Her voice suddenly dropped, carrying a distant air of mystery.
" Only, one pair of creatures could not appear on the deck. "
" Huh? What kind? "
" Snakes. "
His blazing red eyes narrowed with suspicion.
" Why? "
" They had to crawl on their bellies for the sin of tempting mankind. Even so, they managed to lay an egg on the side of the ship in its final hours. "
He whipped half his face around toward her.
" Then that snake is still alive? "
" I don't know, it's just an old tale. No one has ever seen a snake. "
The thread of her story drifted slowly on through the deserted corridor.
" After the days of storms, the waters receded. All living things multiplied until this very day, and the line of mankind was reborn once again. "
The story ended just as their steps reached the barren flower bed. He gently set her down beside the stone ledge. Not a single living sprout clung to the cold stone, only a few withered petals scattered at the bottom of the bed. There was no white flower stem like the one at the market that day.
Kristin gazed at these ruins longer than at any other corner of the sanctuary. The shadow of sorrow lingering in her eyes made his chest sink slightly. She reached out and carefully touched the rotting remnants, as if piecing back together the illusion of her childhood, as if recalling her mother from those years. It was a grief so deep that even one as arrogant as he did not dare pry. He only stood there quietly.
The same petals, the same woman. But the scene before him was gloomier than he wanted to see. He wanted to keep this image forever, and also the remnants that had once belonged to her.
Kristin let the crumbling petals fall back into the stone bed, then looked up, silently, as if claiming the privilege of being carried once more. This time, he put away every sigh and obediently turned his back, lowering himself to take that fragile weight. Yet she seemed suddenly heavier than moments before. He refused to dig deeper to understand, only wanting to square his shoulders and bear it.
Two shadows leaning on each other drifted on along the corridor, past statues of saints gnawed by the fangs of time.
" Dragalon, try turning right. I remember there used to be a library here. "
His step hesitated slightly.
" I do not like places like that. "
" I can tell from your face. "
He froze at her blunt exposure.
" Who told you that? "
" I read people much better than you. "
He lazily ignored her, not bothering to argue, but his steps resignedly changed direction.
The library appeared around a bend. Grey cobwebs were strung densely across every gap of the moldy cabinets. Thousands of books faded and wilted as if forgotten long ago, a few torn volumes scattered across the floor. Kristin slid off his back. She strolled slowly among the enormous groaning shelves, her slender fingertips wiping away the dust that had slept for so many years. Her figure moved through the ashes with a strange ease, and her bond with this place showed clearly in every page she opened.
He stepped back and leaned against a ruined corner of the wall, watching quietly. That moldy pile of scrap paper held no alluring magic. Had it been the him of old days, a few brief seconds could not have held his feet. And yet now, he was willing to be patient.
Her steps slowed more and more, her hands turning over every darkest corner of the wooden shelves, not rummaging at random but as if searching for something she already knew was there.
" Found it! "
Kristin joyfully pulled a thick ancient book out of a narrow gap. The cobwebs tried to cling on, but had to break. Her voice was higher than necessary, and she glanced at him for exactly one beat, as if to see whether he had noticed.
" The Miracle of Pains. "
He narrowed his eyes, reading each letter carved deep into the cover, heavy as a curse. She eagerly opened a page, then hurriedly turned another.
" You are not going to read it now, are you? "
She glanced up at him in a flash, then fixed her eyes on the page again.
" Why not? "
That habit of turning the question around again.
" Then I will go ahead. "
She ignored the childish threat, not taking her eyes off the page.
" You won't do that. "
" What makes you believe I will not? "
The dry rasp of parchment sounded as she calmly turned to the next page.
" Because last night you didn't leave either. "
In that moment, his voice was completely paralyzed. He could not scrape together a single word to argue back, and could only stare in silence at the stubborn woman before him.
Suddenly, her expression went rigid. He keenly caught the change. The lines of text seemed to keep reflecting in those pale pupils.
" Something interesting? "
It took an unusual moment of silence before Kristin managed to squeeze out a sound.
" No, it's nothing. "
The tome suddenly slipped from her thin fingers. She hurriedly bent to pick it up, clutching it tight against her chest. His eyes instantly noticed that the wound on her shoulder had begun to seep blood again.
" Get on. "
" Why does your face look like you don't want to? "
This time, he ignored the stubborn teasing. Her thin arms wrapped around his neck again, The Miracle of Pains pressing against his back.
His ego howled in refusal of everything belonging to this foolish holy place. But his heart firmly refused to let that weak body drag itself along. Standing between two nuisances, he chose the less unpleasant one.
He chose to carry her once more.
