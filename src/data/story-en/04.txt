Each step sank deep into the sodden mud. One step, then another. Nothing remained to make him look back.
The lamps of the quarter were now only a few faint yellow dots, swallowed by the endless white.
Manlanier.
Nothing but a dead, rotting citadel in a thick fog. Grey rooftops appeared and vanished, deep alleys seemed to lead straight into nothingness. His steps grew hurried without his noticing.
" Kristin? "
Her name was trapped in the freezing air. Not a single reply, only the squelch of mud beneath his bare heels.
Was she afraid of him?
The thought flashed and faded quickly. Moments ago she had held his hand tightly, and that fragile warmth did not know how to lie. Then why? And since when did he have to care so much about a human? His legs kept rushing on. Even he could not find a reason for this strange instinct, only that some urge was forcing his body. He wanted to be sure she was still safe.
No. How foolish it sounded. Who would want to linger beside a creature like him?
Drag looked down at the coat she had gone to the trouble of buying him. Its dark hem was still stained with the blood of the men at the tavern. He recalled the moment that small hand had carefully brushed a dead leaf from his shoulder, then opened the hand he had kept clenched since leaving Ditovias.
Four Chromee.
He needed to return the silver she had spent on these clothes. At least, that was an excuse convincing enough for his mind right now. Drag tucked the four coins into the inner fold of his coat and gripped the collar tight.
A biting gust blew through the dark alley, tearing the fog ahead into thin strips. Something lingered within the wind: a scent so very gentle, so very familiar. He had smelled it walking behind her, when she dragged him to Ditovias. The scent of her hair. The scent of the cloth she wore. A scent he had no word to define, except pleasant.
Drag raised his head and took another deep breath. It was still there. He turned on his heel at once, before the fog could swallow the last thin trace of it.
South of Manlanier there was nothing but vast wasteland. A few withered cornstalks loomed faintly, rows of corn rising out of the mist pooled at their roots. He forced his way through row after row, letting the dry leaves scratch his skin. The scent led him to a ragged farmstead: a rickety wooden house, a cattle shed reeking of damp, all penned in by a few rotten, moss-covered fences. He did not bother looking for a way in. One hand lightly on a wooden post, his whole tall frame vaulted over the fence.
But the moment his feet touched the ground, he realized the scent had changed. Still the gentle, familiar smell, but something else was overpowering it: heavier, more rank. It came from something lying no more than three strides away.
A dead sheep. It lay toppled in the crushed grass, its four legs curled, its eye sockets bulging and lifeless. A deep wound ripped from its flank down to its belly, as if it had been gutted by a sickle. Its entrails spilled out, swarming with flies. This pitiful creature had stopped breathing only moments ago.
Right beneath the grass beside the carcass, a thin crack snaked through the soil. No heat rose from it, only a dry gust of cold escaping, carrying a strange smell he had never come across in Manlanier. Drag glanced at it for a moment and moved on. It was not something he needed to mind right now.
" Kristin? "
This time, what answered him came from the wooden house ahead: a dry sound, exactly like someone's footstep on rotting floorboards. He stopped dead.
The wooden door had nearly been torn from its hinges. He slipped through the shattered frame. Inside was a cold expanse of darkness, not a single ray of moonlight getting through the cracks, only a thin layer of dust over the wreckage of furniture. Kristin was not here. He was about to turn around when an extremely faint sound broke the stillness.
Drip.
A drop of liquid fell onto the shoulder of his coat. He reached up to touch it. This thickness was not a leak. Another drop fell. Drag slowly craned his neck back to look up at where that rank liquid was dripping from.
On the rotting wooden beam, a hideous shape hung limp, upside down.
" What the hell are you? "
Sharp, pallid white teeth slowly parted in the thick darkness. The grotesque creature tilted its lumpy head. A pair of enormous ears flared out to either side, their skin wrinkled and thick like the wings of a bloodthirsty bat. Its face had no eyes at all, only a layer of slick, slimy skin stretched across where sight should have been. Perhaps those enormous ears had replaced everything.
Before Drag could assess it further, the creature dropped from the beam.
Crash.
It slammed down on him, its whole weight bearing down on his body. The two bodies crashed to the floor, a few rotten boards beneath his back splintering. He strained his arms to throw it off, but it was heavier than he had thought, twice the fleshy nobleman at Ditovias. The monster opened its jagged maw wide and snapped straight down at his throat. Drag jerked his head aside, and the teeth sank into the floorboard right beside his ear. But luck had never been his companion. The creature yanked its maw up and snapped down a second time.
Thunk.
The fangs pierced his shoulder. A blinding pain tore down his spine, and dark red blood instantly soaked the fur coat. Drag clenched his jaw, his left hand swinging up to seize one of the oversized ears, and pulled back with gritted teeth. It stubbornly refused to let go. He pulled even harder. The slimy skin at the base of the ear swelled and split. Sinewy strands of flesh bulged out, then snapped. One strand. One strand at a time.
Rip.
The monster let out a shrill roar, a deafening sound that pierced his eardrums, and the frame of the whole house shook violently. But Drag absolutely would not let go. His other hand clenched into a fist and slammed into its temple. Its skull was knocked to one side, and its teeth were finally forced out of his mangled shoulder. He drew both legs in, gathered momentum, then kicked with all his strength straight into the middle of the creature's chest.
BOOM.
The colossal body was flung away, plowing through the wooden wall behind it. Splintered boards flew everywhere, dust and sawdust billowing up to fill the whole room.
Drag braced a hand on the floorboards and staggered to his feet. Blood poured from his shoulder down his arm, enough to kill any human. He stared at the stretch of wall that had just been punched through. The creature was not dead, he knew it well. But lingering to fight in this cramped darkness was not a wise move.
He strode outside. The pale moonlight struggled through the fog, just enough for him to make out the shape of the ground ahead. He looked around warily, needing to find the monster before it had another chance to strike from behind. But his eyes stopped on something else.
In the middle of the withered grass lay a dark fur cloak, not torn, not bloodstained, smooth as if it had never been dropped.
Drag stood motionless, as if turned to stone. He recognized that cloth.
" Kristin? "
Whoosh.
A pitch-black shadow flashed past his face, so fast it left nothing behind but a wind that chilled the back of his neck. The familiar shrill screech tore through the air again, this time from the edge of the roof. Drag's head snapped up. Hidden in the darkness, a ghastly white mouth slowly parted, its jagged teeth curling up as if it were smiling. A contemptuous smile reserved for foolish prey.
Suddenly a piercing pain struck to the marrow in his right arm.
" No... "
He looked down in shock. His entire arm from the biceps down had vanished from his body. Not vanished. It lay sprawled a few strides away in the mud, its severed fingers still twitching beat by beat, fresh blood spurting from the ragged stump.
Drag bit his lip until it bled. Now he understood that mocking smile. To this creature, it was only a hunting game for amusement, and he was the toy that entertained it.
The monster spread its wings, a sticky membrane stretched taut from its arms down along its flanks, revealing two sharp, hooked claws. Then it dove. Drag had only just begun to dodge.
Thunk.
The two claws sank deep into both his shoulders. The creature beat its wings, and his body was lifted from the ground. He thrashed and struggled in vain; the two claws were still buried down to the bone.
Then suddenly, the blood stopped flowing.
The streaks of blood seeping from the stump began to twist and crawl back up. They refused to fall to the ground, curling and twining tightly around the severed upper arm. A stark white bone slowly emerged from the mass of blood, stretched out, then crossed into a bony hand at its very end. Five fingers clenched shut, seizing the ankle of the flying creature. The monster flinched. From below, Drag raised his head and stared straight at it.
" I guess you still know nothing about me. "
Bright red bundles of muscle sprouted, wrapping tightly around the newly regrown bone, every strand of flesh, every layer of membrane woven together until whole. But the blood did not only gather where the arm had been severed. Another part of his veins tore itself away from the wound and floated up into the air, wholly defying gravity, a column of deep red blood hovering close to his side. Drag spread his left hand wide. The column of blood obediently flowed into his palm, contracted, and hardened into form, each edge jutting out razor sharp.
A blood sword, forged from his very own flesh and blood.
Drag twisted his wrist coldly. The bright red blade swept across the leg clutching him.
Thunk.
A limb of the beast was severed. Drag let himself fall freely. The wind howled in his ears, but he did not care to glance at the hard ground rushing toward him. At both his shoulders, bloody gashes began to open their mouths. A sharp bone pierced through his flesh, stretched out and split into many keen branches, and clusters of pitch-black feathers sprouted to cover the bones. One by one. One by one they burst open. He had forgotten he still had them. They grew out like a reflex of his body, not his will.
In the blink of an eye, two enormous jet-black wings had spread wide behind him. The ash-grey wings that the base nobles of Ditovias had craved to carve up.
The monster shrieked in pain, then dove after him in fury, its two remaining claws aimed straight at him like a hawk showing off the perfect finishing blow. But the game had been turned on its head. It was no longer master of the hunt. Drag turned leisurely in midair, his black wings gliding with disdain, the corner of his lips curling slightly.
" I've been waiting for you. "
The bloodthirsty creature crashed toward him. Drag thrust his sword straight ahead.
Thunk.
The blood sword pierced through its slimy mouth and plunged deep into its skull. Both bodies slammed into the dry ground at once. The earth shook, mud and weeds exploding upward. The sword was still pinned through the top of the monster's head, but its filthy life force had not yet run dry. Its four limbs twitched, its two claws tearing up the ground in desperation. Drag planted a foot on its chest.
" Where is that woman? "
The creature screeched wildly, so high that his eardrums rang with a piercing ache. Drag tilted his head indifferently.
" Yes. I heard you. "
Both his hands gripped the hilt tightly and yanked down hard.
Rip.
The blade split the misshapen skull from the roof of its mouth down to its throat. The shrill screech was instantly silenced. Its body twitched a few more feeble beats, then sank entirely into death.
Drag pulled out the sword and stood up straight, not bothering to glance at the carcass again. He strode over to Kristin's fur coat and bent to pick it up. Her familiar scent still lingered on the worn cloth, but it was now soiled by the stench of blood. A trail of dark red blood dragged from where the coat had fallen, crawling across the crushed grass and winding toward the back of the sheep pen. He followed the trail at once. One step, then faster. The smell of fresh blood grew stronger and stronger.
Behind the rickety wall of the sheep pen, a fragile body lay motionless in the hay trough. Before his mind could scream anything at all, his legs had already rushed forward on their own.
" Kristin. "
She was buried in silence. Still not a word in reply.
He dropped to his knees, close beside her faint breathing. Bright red streaks of blood still stained the dry straw. He carefully turned her over. A bleeding gash ran from her shoulder down to the middle of her chest. Not deep enough to take her life, he was certain. Only, the edge of the wound was too straight, not like the ragged tears the creature's claws had just left on his own body.
The thought had barely crossed his mind when her faint breathing pulled it away.
He slid one arm under the nape of her neck, his other hand wrapping around her small back, tenderly drawing her body to rest against his chest. Her weak breathing brushed softly across his skin, each breath faint but still clinging on. The corners of her pale eyes trembled slightly. She would still hold on. He comforted himself like that.
Her heavy lashes slowly parted. It was still that faint gold in her left eye, still that haughty gaze from before. But the voice that rose from her throat was more fragile than anything he had ever heard.
" Démon.. I want.. to get out of here. "
That pitiful, ridiculous name again. For an instant, he wished she could call him by his real name. But then a hidden corner of him brushed the thought aside. Better not.
He sank into her eyes. Reflected in their clouded surface was the whole of his form: his blazing red pupils, the pitch-black feathers scattered over his shoulders. Only him. He immediately cast aside every hesitation.
" Yes. I heard you. "
