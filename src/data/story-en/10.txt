Above the Endless Dusk mountains, the sky exploded violently.
A colossal pillar of light tore through the thick clouds and crashed straight down onto the arrogant peak. Boulders shattered, great slabs of mountain thundering down off the edge of the deep chasm.
Drag and Bellia looked up almost at the same moment at the heavens being torn apart by the light. The light was so brilliant that even the twilight around the Endless Dusk was driven aside, revealing a deep blue expanse opening in the middle of the sky. Both of them stood rooted, motionless. Not quite because of the terrifying grandeur descending before their eyes, but because there will always be beauties in this world beyond human understanding. Only when they come close enough do people realize, stunned, that they should begin to tremble.
From behind the broken clouds, an enormous shape gradually revealed itself. Towering ivory walls joined to rows of sacred stone slabs floating in a sea of clouds. Spires rising so high their tips were still lost in the light, and at the very center an enormous golden bell, so large that the patterns on its surface still burned clearly into the eye from dozens of miles away. A holy city, not built on human soil, but majestically, slowly descending from the sky.
Bellia lifted her face. The naive smile that always lived on her face had vanished completely.
" The City of Mercy Dreams. "
Drag turned to look at her. He had never heard that name, but he recognized something else hanging before his eyes. Not the city, the soaring towers or the arrogant golden bell, but a feeling of oppression slowly bearing down on his chest. A gloom he had once tasted at Mother of Mothers, an ancient presence that his body was made to remember before his mind could even name it.
The divine light slowly faded, just enough for another shape beneath the city to be laid bare. An enormous hand of gold reached down from the bottom of the city, its five lifeless fingers slowly clutching the peak of the Endless Dusk, as if heaven had just reached down to the lower world. Not to save it, but to hold it in place.
Drag stared fixedly at that hand. A white void flashed past, tearing through his mind. Cold golden fingers resting on his brow.
" The First Child of The Miracle. "
His breathing grew heavier. Bellia looked over in confusion.
" You know that thing? "
He did not answer. The golden hand on the peak suddenly clenched.
DONG.
The first toll of the bell rang out, but the sound did not merely travel through the air. It pierced the earth, crept through every vein of stone and ran straight into the bones and flesh of every living thing still clinging to existence. All of Cvstodia trembled at once.
Bellia clenched both hands tight.
" It can't be... "
His head snapped toward her. She was looking at the city in the sky with a look he had never seen.
" The Miracle of Pains... "
The ground beneath their feet shook violently. A deep crack yawned open before them, then a second, a third, one after another, ripping through the dry earth. In only a few breaths, pitch-black fissures had crawled across the whole ground like the veins of some gigantic monster. From deep below, a roar rose. It did not belong to a human throat, nor did it resemble a beast, but was like thousands of throats locked together in a single gullet, all tearing at one another at once.
Pitch-black hands reached out of the fissures, claws gripping the earth while pair after pair of bat wings burst open one by one. He had faced this thing once, at the abandoned farm south of Manlanier, the night he followed Kristin's scent. But this time there was not just one. Hundreds, then thousands of Darksiders poured from the earth, blanketing the sky and rushing toward villages, citadels and rooftops that had not yet realized the end of the world had begun. They made no distinction between young and old, soldiers or common folk. Beneath those claws, every living soul was nothing but fleeing lumps of flesh.
Jingle.
The silver earrings on Bellia's right ear trembled violently. She swayed for a beat, then dropped onto one knee.
" Bel? "
Right beneath her feet, the ground suddenly burst open. A grotesque hand seized her ankle from the fissure. He lunged forward before his mind could even think. Blood burst from his palm, clotting into a bright red blade, and slashed down.
Thunk.
The monster's arm was severed. He swept her into his arms, his ash-grey wings bursting open to pull them both off the ground just before the fissure swallowed the place they had stood.
DONG.
The second toll crashed down. Bellia's body jerked hard in his arms, the silver earrings on her right ear glowing red-hot, one beginning to warp. The molten metal ran down the rim of her ear along with blood.
" Please... "
She clutched her head tight.
" Please make it stop. "
He gritted his teeth and held her closer.
" Bel. "
No answer, only her clenched teeth and blood crawling down her neck. He had no idea what to do. One who could regrow bone and flesh, wield his own veins as weapons and survive even with his head severed from his neck, stood helpless before something that had no shape to cut. There was no wound to stitch. No bone to set.
He swept his eyes over the chaotic earth below, trying to find a roof, a mountain, anything that could keep her safe. But at that moment Cvstodia had no place left that looked like safety. At the edge of the horizon, the moon began to swallow the last rays of the sun, and a dark red hue fell over the whole continent. Blood-red lightning tore across the clouds, and the golden hand on the Endless Dusk clenched harder, cracking the land open as boiling lava surged from the deep fissures. A citadel in the distance vanished beneath a sea of fire in only a few beats.
Bellia trembled in his arms.
" They're... "
She struggled to gather each breath.
" They're being burned... "
" Who? "
She did not look at him.
" All of them. "
Another earring shattered. She hurriedly clamped her right ear while the remaining rings shook so hard they tore the rim of her ear.
" They're begging... "
Jingle. Jingle.
DONG.
The last toll crashed down. Bellia screamed.
" I CAN'T TAKE IT ANYMORE! "
Fresh blood gushed from her right ear.
" More and more... More and more people... They're crying... They're screaming... They're... "
She suddenly went silent. Her brown eyes flew wide, as if among the countless sounds crowding inside The Ear, she had just heard a truth more terrifying than all the screams.
" THEY CAN'T DIE! "
He froze in midair.
A Darksider swooped across in front of him. Beneath its claws was a man whose lower half had all but vanished. His entrails trailed in the wind, yet his two arms still flailed desperately.
He was still alive.
A tear rolled from the corner of the man's eye and fell onto the monster's forearm clutching him. Its claws loosened for a beat, as if burned. Then it clenched tight again, and the man screamed once more.
Down on the ground, another who had lost nearly half his face still dragged himself along, trying to flee. A body whose head had been crushed by stone still clawed at the dirt with blood-soaked fingers.
None of them were allowed to die.
He looked down in horror. Through a life so long that even he could no longer remember it, immortality had never been something worth minding. To him, death was only a brief interruption: an arm lost would grow back, a broken heart would beat again, even a head severed from the neck would return. But the people below were not like him. They did not heal, did not regenerate, nor were they released. Their bodies simply went on existing along with everything that had been crushed.
Something in his chest contracted slightly. This was the first time he had seen the shape of the curse he himself had carried all his life, only that the bodies of those people were not merciful enough to mend themselves.
Bellia burst into tears. Tears mixed with blood ran down her right cheek.
" They're praying... "
Her voice shattered.
" They want release. They need... "
She clutched at his shoulder.
" DEATH! "
He looked down at the chaotic world of mankind. Only a few days ago, those very people had knelt low begging to live one more day, praying for the child at home to recover, praying for the one they loved not to stop breathing. Now, those same prayers all turned toward a single longing.
-DEATH-
She bowed her head low, seemingly without the strength left to make a sound. The last earring popped from her right ear and melted into a red-hot drop of silver before it could fall.
A very small movement appeared deep inside her bleeding right ear. A slender white tongue slowly slipped out.
" Bellia? "
The forked tongue lightly swept over the blood-soaked rim of her ear. Right after, a tiny white snake's head wriggled out of her ear. Her body convulsed. Her pallid skin suddenly went dry, dense wrinkles appeared and spread down her neck, and in only a few breaths, she looked as if she had aged decades.
The white snake kept crawling out of her ear. At first it was no larger than a finger, but every length of body that slipped free made it longer and its white scales clearer. It crawled down her cheek, slid around her neck, then coiled once over her thin shoulder. A second coil wrapped around her chest. A third embraced her waist. Her body was almost motionless within the coils of scales.
" Bel? "
Not a single reply. The snake coiled tighter. Its jaw opened to an unnatural size.
He had barely reached out when the white head struck down.
It swallowed her from above.
No blood. No sound of bones breaking. The small body simply vanished slowly into a jaw stretched open absurdly wide.
Her hair disappeared first.
Then her face.
Her shoulders.
Her arms.
Until at last her legs were pulled out of sight.
The white serpent had swallowed her whole.
He stared at the snake's bulging neck, where a shape like a human body slowly slid down inside the scales. The deeper it drifted, the larger the white serpent swelled. Its body stretched dozens of meters, rows of black ancient script beginning to appear along each layer of scales. Its enormous weight dragged him down headlong. He spread his ash-grey wings, straining to hold them both, but the white serpent suddenly thrashed hard.
Below lay an endless dark red lake. The Discrated Lake.
His hands slipped off the scales. He was forced to let go. The snake fell, but the moment a gap opened between their bodies, the white head suddenly whipped back around. A long fang plunged straight through his ankle. Before he could wrench free, his whole body was yanked down by a savage pull.
SPLASH.
---
Dark red, thick and viscous, closed over his head. The light was swallowed whole.
While the fang was still pinned in his ankle, something entirely different began to flood into his head. Not venom, nor a numbness running through his veins. Only human voices. One voice, then ten, a hundred, thousands, until the numbers lost all meaning. Everything The Ear had to hear when no silver was left to protect it flooded into his mind through the fang.
Curses, prayers, a small child calling for its mother, a man begging the one beside him to cut off his head. Someone being burned alive. A mother holding a child no longer whole that was still not allowed to stop breathing. Souls longing to live. Souls begging to die. Some begged for both in a single breath.
He choked. His wings lost their rhythm, letting the white serpent drag him deep into the lake. A sword began to gather in his palm. A single slash and the tail coiled around him would be severed. He knew it. His arm knew it too. But not a single muscle would move.
He let his body sink. The cold water embraced his skin while countless voices kept clawing at his mind. And strangely, amid all that chaos, a sudden relief embraced him. No need to keep fighting. No need to chase anyone, no need to go looking for Kristin or try to hold on to Bellia. No need to remember Krisanta, no need to hate the name Lucifer. No need even to hold on to Dragalon. Only to let go and let this body sink deeper still. The world above would keep turning on its own without him.
Perhaps that was what he had longed for for so long. Not freedom, but a place where nothing forced him to exist anymore.
His eyelids grew heavy. The sword in his hand dissolved into the water.
He was tired.
Not the weariness of muscles, for this immortal body had never been granted that privilege. It always grew back, always dragged him to his feet and forced him to return after every time he was torn apart. He was tired of that. Tired that fate always found another name to call him, another role to stuff him into.
Lucifer. Dragalon. The First Child. Apocalypse Warrior.
Tired of having only just learned how to hold on to someone, only to immediately watch that person take up a sword on the other side. Tired that the names he had begun to cherish all seemed to have been written before he even had the chance to meet them.
Kristin. Krisanta. Bellia. Bel'vi.
Those names drifted among the red water. Perhaps he had never truly owned a name. He himself had never even had the right to own himself.
He closed his eyes, letting his body keep sinking. Then, among tens of thousands of screams, a naive voice slipped in. Not because it was louder than the others, but because he recognized it.
" If the one you mean disappears... "
His red eyes flew open.
" Will everyone's pain disappear too? "
Bellia.
Another afternoon beneath the old tree, when her fingers were still gently stroking a few yellowed leaves and the silver earrings were still strong enough to make the world smaller. Then his own words echoed back.
" I will try. "
He stared fixedly into the pitch-black darkness.
Yes. He had said that. Words so light that it seemed a single gust of wind could carry them away. But he had said them.
His hand clenched. The sword appeared once more, pitch-black hellfire crawling along its blade. He turned and plunged the sword down into the white scales. The white serpent thrashed hard, but it was not a killing blow. He only pinned the blade into its body to hold on to himself.
The creature shot through the depths of the lake, the water yanking his body backward. He summoned another sword and plunged it in ahead, then pulled out the other and drove it in higher still. The two blades became hooks, helping him crawl inch by inch along the white serpent's body. The crying in his head grew louder and louder, but he no longer fought it.
How long had Bellia had to hear these sounds? Since she was little, even before she could speak. If she could live with them her whole life, he could surely grit his teeth and endure a little longer.
He crawled closer to its head. The white serpent thrashed so violently he was nearly swept off its scales. He held one hilt tight and pressed his other hand against the snake's body. Something Bellia had said surfaced: only when she touched someone did their thoughts take more shape. He did not know whether the reverse existed. But he had no other way.
His palm pressed hard against the cold scales.
-Bellia-
The name could not escape his mouth in the water. He could only scream it in his mind.
-Do you hear me?-
The rows of ancient script beneath his palm suddenly flashed. The white serpent kept thrashing, but he gritted his teeth and held on.
-I hear it now-
The things you still have to hear.
-I hear all of it-
The snake's body slowed a little. He leaned in close, pressing his forehead to the cold scales.
-I understand now-
A very small silence appeared. Not because the voices had stopped, but because for one brief beat, the white serpent stopped thrashing.
-I'm sorry, Bel-
The creature slowed.
-I don't want you to hear these things alone-
-I know you're tired-
He closed his eyes.
-I'm tired too-
-So tired-
A confession that, had he still been sitting beneath that tree, he would rather have bitten off his tongue than let slip from his mouth. But at the bottom of the lake, with the crying of an entire continent crowding inside his head, pride suddenly became something far too small to keep clutching.
-But I need you-
-I need you to help me-
The rows of ancient script on the scales glowed brighter.
-And I promise-
He paused for a beat. It was not a promise to curse heaven, nor meant to threaten an enemy. Only words meant for the living soul right before him.
-I'll try-
-These voices, this pain... I don't know what to do-
-But I'll try, I swear-
The white serpent stopped thrashing completely. He looked at the white scales before his eyes. An ancient name slipped through his mind.
-Bel'vi-
It was no longer a name he had accidentally dug out of chaotic fragments of memory. It was the thing that had left Bellia frozen the first time she heard him call it, a name she seemed to have buried very deep beneath the form he knew. He pressed his hand harder, as if to be sure she truly heard.
-Bel'vi-
The whole body of the white serpent froze. The rows of ancient script all blazed at once beneath the red water. Then the scales under his hand began to move. Not contracting, but separating from the flesh beneath. A thin crack ran along the snake's body, then another. The enormous white skin wrinkled, dried, then peeled away from the body inside like rotten cloth.
The white serpent was shedding its skin.
He held on tight as each patch of old skin curled up and floated in the water. The snake's head split lengthwise from its snout. Both sides of the scales slowly opened. A human hand pushed through the gap from inside, slender fingers gripping the white husk and pulling it hard to one side. A small shoulder appeared, then a familiar face.
Bellia.
No.
Bel'vi.
She slowly slipped free of the enormous white serpent's husk. The patches of white skin drifted away toward the bottom of the lake like the shell of a creature that had completed its final task. The body beneath still bore a human form. But at her right ear, the drops of silver left from the old earrings began to tremble, crawling along the rim of her ear like drops of living mercury, gathering, stretching, then bending into small finger joints. A silver hand gradually took shape just outside her right ear. It did not grow from her flesh, did not replace any part of her body, only hovered close around the rim of her ear as if to replace the delicate earrings.
Five silver fingers closed gently. Covering The Ear.
He still clung to the disintegrating husk. The strength in his fingers was nearly spent. One finger began to slip. Then a second. Just before he fell completely from the snake's skin, Bel'vi's real hand caught his wrist.
She pulled him back.
He opened his eyes and looked at her. No sound could exist between them. But the moment her hand touched his skin, a soft voice appeared directly in his mind.
-If you don't hate that name...-
He was utterly drained. Her eyes did not leave him.
-Please call me that-
Not words. Not a single bubble escaped her lips. Only that thought, carried by The Ear straight through the place where their bodies touched.
He looked at her for a long time. He did not think the name was beautiful or ugly, nor did he understand why she was afraid of others calling it. But one thing he knew: it was the name she had just chosen for herself.
-Yes, I hear you-
A thought answered before his eyes slowly dimmed.
-Bel'vi-
Her eyes flew wide. Then a very small smile appeared. She gripped his wrist tight and began to pull upward. His body went slack, as if he no longer had the strength to fight the water. And this time, he did not need to fight it.
A glimmer of faith, given to Bel'vi. Or perhaps, given to himself.
---
Two figures broke through the surface of the lake. Cold air flooded into straining lungs while ruined Manlanier appeared faintly in the distance.
When consciousness dimly returned, he realized he was lying on the shore and Bel'vi was still sitting beside him. Her hand had kept holding his fingers ever since the bottom of the lake. The silver hand of the Mark quietly cradled her right ear like a cold ornament. He did not open his eyes, but still heard her voice clearly.
" Thank you. "
A peaceful silence passed.
" For listening with me. "
He did not move. She only gazed silently out at the dark red lake.
" The people out there are so loud. They're still crying, still screaming, and then praying again. "
Her small hand tightened slightly.
" But when you touch me... there's something very different. "
" Different how? "
" There's a voice. Very still. "
A cold wind swept over the Discrated Lake, rippling the murky red water into small rings. She looked down at their two hands holding each other.
" Like the lake out there. I don't know what's beneath it. Maybe I shouldn't know either. "
He slowly opened his eyes. Above the lake, the pitch-black tear in the clouds still stretched on like a wound that could never be stitched. He looked at it for a long time, an emptiness that had clearly once held something, though now he no longer knew what name to call the part that had disappeared.
Bel'vi still sat beside him. The Ear had never stopped hearing the voices of Cvstodia. Those nearby rang clear as screams right beside her ear, while those far away were only endless whispers. But her hand had never let go of him.
He closed his eyes again. He did not know how much longer he lay like that, a few minutes or perhaps longer. When he opened his eyes again, she was still there. The last rays of the sun had all but vanished. White ash began to fall from the sky, gently, like the first snow of the season.
Among the countless flakes of ash, a small white thing suddenly drifted down. He reached out to catch it. A pure white carnation petal lay in the middle of his palm, a streak of dried blood at its edge. He did not need to ask whose blood it was. He remembered the last petal in the rain, and the white wing that was cut away.
His fingers slowly closed.
Bel'vi turned toward him. His eyes still gazed distantly at the sky.
" Kristin. "
She nodded slightly.
" I know. "
He frowned at her.
" You are listening to me again? "
She burst out laughing.
" Sometimes you don't even need to say it out loud. "
He looked at her. The last time, when Bellia had accidentally revealed that she could hear the name Kristin in his mind, his first instinct had been suspicion, then readiness to turn that suspicion into violence. This time he only let out a breath. He was too tired to build another wall. And after hearing the pain of the whole world together with someone, a few private thoughts suddenly seemed not so worth hiding anymore.
She pushed herself up and held out five slender fingers before him.
" I'll go with you. "
He looked up.
" Why? "
She smiled.
" Because I want to hear what she has to say too. "
He looked at that small hand for a long time. A hand that had once trembled so badly it could not hold a tiny earring, yet that very hand had just pulled him from the bottom of the lake. Then he suddenly remembered another hand, smaller, that had once grabbed him in the middle of Manlanier and willfully dragged him to Ditovias. Back then he had not understood why he followed her. He told himself it was only to return four Chromee, a stupid excuse.
Now there was another hand held out before him. But this time no one was dragging him along, no one was holding him back, and there was no prophecy or Mark forcing him to choose. No hand descending from the sky to point him down the road he must walk.
Only him.
He slowly raised his hand.
And took it.
" Yes. "
