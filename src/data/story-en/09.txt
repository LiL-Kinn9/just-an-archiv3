" Lucifer. "
A familiar voice echoed through the boundless void.
" Do you know what love is, my child? "
His eyes flew open.
No more grey sky writhing in lament. No more rotting earth beneath sinful heels. Only a stark white, hollow, stretching to where sight could not reach. The voice crept everywhere, gentle, as if its hand had never pierced through his body, as if that very softness were enough to wash away every torment it had ever sown.
" I fear. "
A breathless silence.
" You never will. "
Drag stepped forward sullenly. The voice instantly slipped away, retreating into the unseen. He strode faster, and it grew fainter still.
He broke into a headlong run.
Each furious step dragged the white along, rolling it into a thick, viscous fog, and in the fog dark brown stone roofs took shape, rough cobbled roads, sallow lamps gasping their last breaths.
Manlanier.
But the city was dead.
No cries of vendors, no shops crowding and clinging to one another, no foul smell of food that he had once arrogantly scorned. Before, his ego would surely have longed to sink into this emptiness. Now that deathly silence made the whole city look like one enormous mass grave. The fog swallowed the long, deep streets, and withered, shredded carnation petals lay scattered in broken trails beneath his bare feet.
He halted.
" Kristin? "
Not a single reply. He walked faster, impatient.
" Kristin. "
The name struck hopelessly against the walls of the deep alleys, then echoed back, shredding his heart.
" Kristin... "
A small figure appeared behind the murky white fog, in that familiar dark fur coat. He stood frozen like a statue. She slowly turned around. A pale gold eye, gloomy to the point of haunting, and red lips curving softly into a thin smile.
He lunged forward like a madman, heedless of right or wrong, without the slightest guard, not even asking whether the lying shadow before him was real. A single breath later, he stood towering before her, his hand pinning that small wrist.
Warm.
He gripped tighter still, almost frenzied. The warmth spread until his chest ached again and again, because he had learned a cruel truth: the warmest things in this world can also evaporate in the blink of an eye.
" I think I need to eat something. "
Words so ordinary they chilled him, as if the Ditovias tavern had never been drenched in blood, as if the golden sword had never pierced his heart.
" And what about you? "
His dry lips trembled slightly.
" Kristin. "
She tilted her head innocently.
" I do not need to eat anymore. "
He kept clutching that hand, refusing every thought of letting go, as if loosening a single knuckle would let her slip from his hand once more.
" I feel cold. "
She did not answer. The smile was still on her lips, but the warmth in his palm was draining away, into thin air. He looked down in panic. Her skin was slowly turning deathly pale, lifeless.
Cold as a corpse.
" Kristin? "
Not a single reply.
" I want to leave this place. "
His head snapped up. Her voice had changed, strange and hollow.
" But perhaps I shouldn't. "
Her face blurred, melting into the fog. He gritted his teeth, pouring all his strength into his hand. The smile faded right before his eyes.
" I think I shouldn't see you anymore. "
The hand in his turned into a thin, cold wisp of mist.
He grabbed at it.
Nothing to hold.
He snatched again, frantic. Nothingness slipped through his fingers.
The third time, he caught only air that froze to the bone.
He stared blankly down at his empty palm. Blood was seeping out. His nails had dug deep into his own flesh at some point.
Manlanier evaporated around him: stone roofs, flickering street lamps, withered carnation bushes, all swallowed by the fog. At last this realm came to an end.
Only he remained. Alone in the abyss.
The loneliness he had once arrogantly believed held nothing to fear now stretched the void out endlessly. The blood in his veins seemed to stop flowing. A very long time, it seemed like centuries. This time he bit his lip to shreds and no longer called her name. Perhaps his ego had finally, bitterly admitted that the name did not hold enough magic to keep someone from leaving.
The bell of the rotting church suddenly pealed, deafening. An invisible blade tore through the wind straight at his chest.
---
His eyes snapped open.
Crows fled in panic from the treetops before him. Blinding sunlight crashed down, searing his eyes, and an unfamiliar face was leaning close in curiosity.
" Hello. "
He jerked back on reflex. The girl also stumbled back a few steps in alarm.
" Oh. You're really alive. "
She tilted her head and stared at him with an innocent nosiness.
" What's your name? "
He stared blankly at the face blocking his view. His twisted mind had not yet torn itself free of the nightmare's abyss.
" Kristin?! "
The girl blinked her doe eyes.
" You've got a girl's name, huh? Or are you calling me? "
" No. "
" Good. Because my name isn't Kristin. "
She pointed a finger at her own smudged cheek.
" Bellia. Nice to meet you. "
Her eyes slid down his body, her brows lifting in embarrassment.
" But I think you should... I don't know. Get off the tree first. "
He followed her hesitant gaze. A pitch-black, gnarled branch was piercing through his chest, its sharp tip jutting right out the front. The blood had nearly half clotted.
" It's going right through you. "
Her voice was strangely light, as if reminding him that a fallen leaf was caught on his shirt. He looked at the branch, then at her. Then he calmly grasped the wooden branch and snapped it.
Crack.
One hand gripping the broken stump, he pulled himself free of that wooden spear. Blood poured onto the green grass. Bellia stood staring as the shredded strands of muscle frantically clung to one another, knitting back seamlessly.
" Oh. You can heal. "
He did not answer.
" Pretty cool. "
He narrowed his eyes at her. A young girl with a wild air about her. Tangled hair the color of mud, clear, pale brown eyes. Both her arms were covered in tattoos of ancient script and patterns winding like vines. Countless metal rings hung from both her ears, some piercing right through the thin rim, some linked to one another by fine silver threads.
She too examined him without hesitation. From his angular face down to his bloodied chest, then sliding lower still. Her face suddenly flushed red, and she hurriedly turned away.
" Where are your clothes? "
He looked down at his naked body. The armor had turned to ash among the clouds. He had nothing left to wear, and had never seen it as a problem. Bellia bit her lip hard, her shoulders shaking.
" Are you laughing? "
" No. "
She turned her whole body away to hide.
" Not at all. "
A giggle slipped out of time from between her teeth.
" I heard that. "
" I'm sorry! "
She scrambled off toward the small, ramshackle hut.
" I've got some old trousers! Don't know if they'll fit you. But fitting or not, it's still better than this! "
The wooden door creaked shut.
He stood in the middle of a garden brimming with life. Plump watermelons lay everywhere, wildflowers grew in bright rows tucked beside the wooden house, the wild wind caressing the grass and plants. A white petal was swept up and caught beneath his foot. He looked down.
A carnation.
He stood looking at it longer than a lifeless petal deserved anyone's attention. In his mind, another white petal was slowly being stained in a pool of blood. He nudged it aside with his toe, very gently, just enough force, so as not to crush the fragile petal.
The door burst open. Something came flying and covered his whole head. He did not dodge.
" You really weren't going to dodge? "
He pulled the coarse fabric down in irritation. Bellia was already leaning against the doorframe, a bright red slice of watermelon in one hand, still munching.
" Are you hungry? "
" No. "
" I only have watermelon. But it's really good. I grew it myself. "
He gritted his teeth and pulled on the ragged trousers. They stopped at his ankles; he decided not to comment. Then he dropped down dazedly at the foot of an old tree nearby, leaning back against its rough trunk. He looked up at the sky. A blue sky so false it was unpleasant. And among the clouds, a pitch-black tear split the sky into two mournful halves. His own slash. The memory of the void that had just swallowed him rushed back, gnawing at his soul. He closed his eyes.
Before long, he was disturbed. Bellia was bending over in front of him, holding out a juicy red slice of watermelon.
" Here. "
He stayed as unmoving as stone.
" Eat it. "
He stayed still.
" My arm's getting tired. "
He took it in irritation. He told himself it was only to make her stop nagging. Bellia plopped down on the grass beside him. She did not ask where he came from, did not ask why his chest had just been impaled by a tree, did not even wonder why he had fallen naked into her garden. She only sat there like a small statue. That quiet was not stifling like the silence in the nightmare; it did not make his mind scream that he was being thrown away.
He took a bite of the watermelon. Sweet. The sweetness flashed across his tongue and vanished.
" Is it good? "
" Not really. "
She turned to glare at him in a sulk.
" Liar. "
He ignored her and kept chewing the bland melon.
" If it's not good, why do you keep eating? "
" I like things that taste bad. "
She blinked.
" Eh? Am I forcing you that much? "
" Yes. "
" But... "
" You talk too much, do you not? I do not want to talk. "
She puffed out her cheeks and pouted with a sigh.
" Yes sir... "
She obediently propped her hands behind her, gazing up at the sky with him, and truly fell silent. A minute passed. Two minutes. He glanced over in suspicion. She was not restless, nor did she come up with some new prying question to bother him, a strange thing he had never seen in a human. He looked down at the half-eaten slice in his hand.
" You live here alone? "
She burst out laughing, turning to look at this contradictory man.
" You just said you didn't want to talk. "
" I changed my mind. "
" Mm. I live alone. "
He swept his eyes over the quiet garden.
" How does it feel? "
" What? "
" Being alone. "
She wrinkled her forehead and thought for a while.
" Pretty great. "
" You are lying. "
" No. "
She pointed carelessly all around the garden.
" I've got my little watermelons. My little flowers. And a few birds who love stealing my seeds. I'm not alone at all. "
She tilted her head, looking deep into his empty eyes.
" But you look kind of lonely, huh? "
He bowed his head.
" What is lonely? "
A question foolish to the point of honesty. He knew well the smell of pain, could easily feel hatred, knew by heart the longing to tear a creature apart. But there were still too many human feelings he had never had the chance to name.
This time she did not laugh at him. She only stayed silent and waited patiently. A white petal was swept past by the wind, brushing his cheek.
" A little. "
Very quiet, a whisper, almost a confession that had accidentally fallen from his mouth. She did not offer empty comfort, did not tell him everything would be fine, did not try to mend something she did not even know where it had broken. She only kept sitting quietly beside his enormous shadow.
He took another bite of watermelon.
" But thank you for asking. "
She smiled, a smile as clear as morning light.
" You're welcome. "
---
The distant sky slowly turned orange. A god and a human still sat at the foot of the old tree. He suddenly broke the stillness.
" Do you know what love is? "
She widened her pale brown eyes.
" Love? "
She stared at the slice of watermelon dwindling in his hand.
" Can you eat it? "
He snapped his head around, brows knitted tight. She hurried to giggle and soothe him.
" I'm joking. "
He turned back wearily to the sunset. A damned question that He had once used to curse and imprison him, and now he was taking it to ask a melon-growing girl in the middle of nowhere.
She curled up, hugging both knees to her chest.
" I think... "
She pondered for a long time.
" I love everything here. I love watermelons. I love flowers. I love that tiny little house. "
She opened her hands and looked at her palms, smudged with mud.
" And I love myself too. "
" Love yourself? "
" Mm. It's not hating this body just because of a few ugly scars. Not hating your own thoughts just because sometimes they're stupid. Not throwing away the things that belong to you just because other people don't like them. "
She stopped talking. No lecture came with it. She only bent to brush a little soil off her knees, then went on sitting beside him.
He fell silent. The ash-grey wings of disgrace rose mockingly in his mind. The pools of blood. The treacherous sword. That damned name, Lucifer. All the filthy things he had considered the most repulsive remains of that rotten sky. Then his thoughts pulled him toward something else: the shabby coat Kristin had gone to the trouble of buying him, now lying somewhere in the ruined church, along with the apple in its bulging fold. A small, lowly thing, so ordinary it could be thrown away. But it belonged to him.
" Cherish the things that belong to you. "
She spoke slowly, stressing each word.
He looked down at his bloodstained hands. Since the dawn of creation, he had never had any notion of what loving oneself was. But for the first time in this tasteless immortality, he began to kindle the thought that perhaps being forced to exist among mankind did not always have to be a torment.
Bellia's smile suddenly died out. She tilted her head, straining her ears as if trying to hear an eerie sound echoing from very far away. He frowned.
" What are you listening to? "
She did not answer. One of the metal rings on her ear trembled violently.
Jingle.
Her trembling hand touched it.
" Kristin. "
His muscles went taut.
" What? "
She fixed her pale brown eyes on him.
" The woman with eyes the color of dusk. "
Every illusion of peace that had just begun to form in him was strangled, stillborn. He turned his massive body fully toward her.
" How do you know her? "
She stayed calm.
" I heard you calling her. "
" I only said a name. How do you know the color of those eyes?! "
" I know. "
He sprang up like a beast driven into a corner. The watermelon fell onto the grass. The old animal instinct rushed back and seized him. Guard. Suspicion. The psychological armor he had so foolishly allowed himself to take off beside Kristin had made him pay a bloody price. He swore a bitter oath never to make the same mistake twice.
" Who are you? "
She let out a long sigh.
" I told you. Bellia. Real name. "
She suddenly fell silent, biting her lip hard. The metal rings on her ear trembled harder.
Jingle. Jingle.
She squeezed her eyes shut, her face crumpling in pain. A drop of thick, dark blood seeped from inside her ear and ran down. She quickly wiped it away.
" It's coming again... "
" What is? "
She buried her head in her knees.
" The whispers. "
A cold wind gusted across the wild field. A metal earring snapped and fell to the ground with a thud. At once she curled up as if a needle had pierced her eardrum, both pale hands clamped over her right ear, her breath coming fast. He stepped closer warily. The sharp, cold guard had not yet left his red eyes, but something invisible was overpowering it.
" What is wrong with you? "
She frantically reached for the ring rolling in the grass, but her fingers shook so hard they could not hold the tiny piece of metal. He let out a breath, bent down to pick it up, and carefully placed it in the middle of her twitching palm. She sank down onto the ground, fumbling to hook the earring back into her bleeding ear. He watched coldly. The suffering of the weak had once not been worth a glance in his dictionary. And yet now, his mind twisted in discomfort, only because the hands of a stranger were trembling before him.
" What is that thing? "
She touched her bloodstained fingers to the cold metal rings.
" They make everything smaller. "
" What gets smaller? "
She turned a distant gaze toward shabby Manlanier.
" Them. The people in the city. Or even farther. They've never stopped talking to me. "
She tried to squeeze out a twisted smile, ten thousand times more pitiful and weary than the smile from before.
" There's nothing happy about it. Just curses. Just insults. Strange, isn't it? "
She looked down at her hands, smeared with blood and mud.
" I live far from everyone because I can't bear them. But when I can't hear anything... "
The forced smile faded, then dissolved completely.
" I start to think they've disappeared. "
He slowly lowered himself and sat down in front of her. He began to faintly understand. She was not afraid of those demonic voices. She was afraid both of their relentless presence and of their hollow absence. And strangely, he himself was trying to lower himself to understand what she said.
" How long have you heard them? "
" I don't remember. Since I was little. Maybe since before I could talk. "
A heavy silence settled over them.
" They have never stopped? "
She shook her head limply.
" Never. "
He looked at the lifeless metal rings, then unconsciously raised a hand. But reason made his right arm stop right before her bleeding ear. Do not touch. His arrogant mind seemed to have begun learning a lesson: not everything he was curious about was his to touch roughly.
" Does it hurt? "
She let out a bitter laugh.
" You're asking me? "
" Answer. "
She raised her brimming eyes to him.
" Yes. "
He slowly lowered his arm, saying nothing more. But a frivolous thought had taken root in him: he hated having to watch her gnaw on that pain. Just as he himself had to endure similar wounds. His ego immediately swerved to another question.
" Can you hear what I think? "
" I can. But not completely. Only when I touch you do the thoughts take more shape. "
" Really? "
She held out her thin hand.
" Can I try touching you? "
" No! "
She laughed clearly, dispelling the gloom.
" Then I'll take my earrings off. "
" No. Do not do that. "
She tilted her head and looked at him in amusement.
" Are you worried about me? "
He snapped around and stared at the lifeless garden.
" I... am not. "
A clumsy lie, even worse than the lie he had just told about the taste of the watermelon.
" Do not do stupid things. "
She smiled gently.
" I'm only teasing you. "
She carefully touched her fingertips to the blood-smeared earrings.
" Sometimes I want it to stop. And sometimes I want them to stop. If they didn't hurt anymore, how good that would be. "
He looked hard at her.
" Why should you care about those people? "
" Because they don't really want to be like this either. "
He raised his red eyes to the sky. The pitch-black tear among the grey clouds was still there, a crack that could never be mended, in the sky that had once been his cage. He looked down at his own bloodstained hand, the hand that had once pointed a sharp sword at fragile Kristin. Not because he longed to take her life. But it had carried out that treacherous act. He remembered clearly the wildness in his eyes at that moment, or more precisely, he did not have a single clear fragment of memory. And it was that hollow, uncontrolled truth that was the nightmare that made even one like him hesitate.
" I do not want it to be like this either. "
She fixed her eyes on him.
" I only want that one to disappear. "
" Who? "
He hurled his hatred straight up at the sky.
" The thing they call a god. The rotten faith they give to that one. "
She laced her hands together, squeezing tight, fidgeting with each thin knuckle.
" If the one you mean disappears... "
She looked down at the dusty earth.
" Will everyone's pain disappear too? "
He could not answer right away. Before, he would not have bothered to care about the pain of the vermin of the lower world. But now he had seen with his own eyes Kristin bleeding, seen Bellia tormented by voices, and dimly understood that the pain of this world was not merely flesh being torn from bone.
" I do not know either, Bellia. "
She bowed her face completely. Her trembling fingers unconsciously stroked the fallen yellow leaves.
" I will try. "
Her head shot up, her eyes resolute.
" Then I'll help you. "
He looked at the dry leaves she was cherishing in vain.
" I do not need your help. "
" Why? "
" I do not know. "
An honest answer, naked to the point of heartbreak, so rare and foreign that even he felt stifled.
" I just do not want it. "
She boldly reached out and lightly touched his shoulder. The silver earrings swayed slightly.
Jingle.
" Because you're afraid you'll lose yourself again? "
He glared at her, not caring when that hand had come to rest on his shoulder. A question that bored into the very depths of his soul, one he never wanted to hear, because its venom stung too close to the truth he was trying to hide.
" Yes. "
A short, dry laugh at himself slipped unbidden from his mouth, so short he did not even notice he had made the sound. She heard it. But she tactfully chose to stay silent.
For the first time since waking with a start beneath the tree, he felt his muscles stop straining. Just a little. He told himself it was only weariness. A god and a human went on sitting together beneath the ragged tree, the orange-gold dusk embracing the rows of wildflowers.
---
She wearily pushed herself up.
" I'll get more watermelon. "
" I do not like watermelon. "
She narrowed her eyes at the green rind lying on the ground, then pointed her foot straight at that evidence of guilt.
" You're a worse liar than I thought. "
She burst out in a crisp laugh, then skipped back toward the wooden house.
" Do you want the big piece or the sm...? "
Her clear, ringing voice grew smaller, then choked off. Not because she stopped talking on her own, but because another sound was strangling it, cutting into reality.
A calm, deep voice.
Familiar.
The very same sound that had called his name in those deadly memories. It did not belong to this world.
She froze and turned back to look at him.
" Do you want the big piece or the small one? "
Two voices tangled, overlapping each other, deafening.
" Lucifer? "
He stood rigid as stone. Every fragile bit of ease that had just appeared was strangled at once. Since waking in that pool of blood, he had never given that shameful name to her, never mentioned it, never even thought of mentioning it.
His red eyes changed.
Cold.
Sharp as a blade.
Ready to turn any wrong answer into a reeking pool of blood.
She stood rooted before the door of her house. The clear smile had evaporated.
Jingle.
Blood began to flow again from that small ear. She still blankly raised the watermelon to her mouth and bit, her other hand gently wiping away the blood crawling down her neck, calm enough to chill the blood, as if this suffering had become so familiar it no longer made her tremble. She looked anxiously deep into those bloodthirsty eyes.
" I think you're about to kill me, huh? "
He gritted his teeth and did not answer. Another forbidden name was stirring, rising to gnaw at his mind, not from a passing thought but from a darker pit, where the fragments of memory about Him had been buried alive under layer upon layer of hatred.
" Bel... "
Her pupils twitched again and again.
" What is it? "
" ...vi. "
She turned to stone at the name that had just slipped unintentionally from his mouth.
That deep, solemn voice echoed back once more. Not her voice, nor his. A stark white, blinding sky flashed past, striking straight into his mind. A colossal heart covering the whole vault of heaven. Three small figures kneeling low in submission.
" Bel'vi. "
He clenched his hand into a fist. The memory pressed deep, nailing itself into his brain.
" Keep this... "
The earrings on her ear trembled harder, more violently.
Jingle. Jingle.
" ...ear. "
He glared at her with hatred, from the rows of ancient script tattooed on her arms, the silver rings binding her ears, to the blood streaming down her cheek, to her ability to hear sounds that no one else could. The scattered fragments began to fit together, roughly locking into one another. A divine, forbidden name appeared. Old. Rotten. So familiar that he bit his lips until they bruised.
-The Ear of The Silent Sorrows-
He held his breath, refusing to say it outright. He was not ready. His chaotic memories had betrayed him too many times. He did not know whether it was the truth he had just recalled on his own, or whether He was pulling the strings from above, wanting to feed him the memory.
She looked at him in confusion.
" What's wrong with you? "
He did not answer. His bloodshot red eyes stayed fixed only on the bleeding earrings. Only a moment ago, in his eyes she had been nothing but a naive country girl who grew watermelons, the only creature who could sit quietly beside him without forcing him to open his mouth, the only person brave enough to ask him whether he was lonely. But now, another ancient name had taken shape behind Bellia's shadow.
Bel'vi.
