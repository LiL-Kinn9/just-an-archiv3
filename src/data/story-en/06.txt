" Some things will not stay with us forever. But some things will stay within us forever. It's a pity that people never know. Or ever knew they existed. "
" Four horsemen. Four horses. Four calamities. Before the storm comes. They always stay until the very last hours. "
He asked.
" But who are they? "
And she answered.
" No one at all. "
Each heavy step trod on the shards of broken glass across the cold bricks. Her voice drifted through the gloomy corridors of the sanctuary, telling old tales that no one remembered anymore. He did not understand why he wanted so much to hear such ordinary sounds. He told himself it was only the last feeble warmth of an autumn sun, an illusion he wanted to keep a little longer, and then let go.
Kristin tilted her head, resting her thin cheek on his shoulder. Her warm breath brushed rhythmically against the hollow of his neck. Drag's steps slowed without his meaning them to.
" Or will you take me back to the chapel? "
" No. I do not want to. "
" I don't care. But I want to. "
He grimaced. He knew well that arguing further would never win against this woman's stubbornness. Yet bowing his head to her wishes did not bring the fury he had always expected.
Two bodies leaning on each other returned to the room thick with incense. The feeble morning light crept through the web of cracks in the stone walls, pale strips of light cutting across the dusty air. Drag stopped before the long wooden bench beside the altar and bent down. Kristin slowly slid off his shoulders. But her slender arms still lingered, loosely wrapped around his neck.
A moment.
Then another moment.
" Why have you not let go? "
Kristin did not answer. She looked up, straight into his blazing red pupils. Her lips parted slightly, her breath rising and falling against his skin. That look again. His face again, trapped in the depths of her eyes, and only his image. At some point, Drag's rough hands had come to rest fully on her slender waist.
" Dragalon. "
" Yes? "
" What are you doing? "
A question that seemed so very simple. But in that suffocating moment, it was the hardest question she had ever asked him. Kristin's smooth hand pressed against the nape of his neck, drawing that face closer still. Her voice rose, thin and low.
" I'm asking. What are you doing? "
Drag turned his gaze to the fresco of a woman holding a lamb. Looking at a painted face was easier than looking at her. The interrogation in her eyes was sharper than ten thousand curses, and he needed to hide feelings that even this body had not yet understood. But Kristin reached up and touched his cheek, forcing that gaze to come back.
" I do not know. "
The corners of her lips curved. A laugh escaped her, very softly.
" You're finally telling the truth. "
He tightened his arms, pulling her flush against his chest.
" What will you do next? "
" Lock your mouth shut. "
The last distance between their bodies was crushed.
Her lips were softer and warmer than anything he had imagined, a primal hunger he had never had the words to name. She leaned back onto the wooden bench behind her, and he was swept along. One of his hands pinned her waist, the other tracing down each vertebra of her back. The warmth of her skin seeped through the thin fabric. Drag no longer remembered how sick this room, reeking of the divine, had once made him.
Kristin bit lightly on his lip, not enough to draw blood, just enough for a beat of numbness. A teasing warning, or the last bit of pride she was still trying to keep. But Drag did not let her go. He growled low in his throat and lifted her up. Her back met the ice-cold stone wall behind her, and between the cold and his heat, she shivered slightly. He saw the reaction, but he did not stop to question it.
His lips found hers again. His hand traveled from her thigh to her small waist, then up to her breast rising and falling beneath her shirt. Kristin answered by biting harder, until his lip bled. Drag frowned. But in that very moment, he was enthralled by the way she fought back. A chain of troublesome, chaotic feelings, and he no longer wanted to waste his mind thinking any further.
For one beat she closed her eyes, and her face suddenly grew lighter, like someone who had just set down something very heavy carried for too long. He thought it was surrender.
Her hand slowly loosened from his shoulder, lowered, and pressed against his chest, resting right over his heart. Her fingertips lingered a beat longer.
Drag only thought she was listening.
" Your heart. "
All his movements stopped.
" What? "
" It's so fast. "
Thump-thump.
Thump-thump.
The sound pounded through every vein, so loud he thought she could hear every pitiful beat of it clearly. He loathed it. He hated that this body was confessing for him things he had never allowed to be laid bare. But his muscles betrayed him, refusing to throw that small hand off. Kristin's fingertips pressed deep into the muscle of his chest, as if wanting to remember its place exactly.
Then those fingertips stopped.
He glimpsed something sway in the depths of her eyes. A little confusion. A little something very close to worry.
But he was too infatuated to name it in time.
Thump-thump.
Thump-thump.
Drag bent down, his lips sliding along her pale neck. Kristin tilted her head to receive him. A very small, inviting movement, but enough for his instincts to understand. He let go of all restraint. Heedless of the cold corridors, unbothered by the distant sounds echoing in, sweeping away the whole stifling mess of memories that had disgusted him when he stepped into this sanctuary. The world held only her: tangled breaths, burning skin, and a heartbeat that refused to obey reason.
That was the moment his pride fell. Not because he was weaker than a human. Only that he himself had taken off every layer of armor.
Kristin's hand was still pressed tight on his chest. Her other hand slowly slid lower. Drag did not resist. He only pulled her even tighter into himself. The mortal. The one who reigned over the heavens. Every boundary between them was erased from his mind.
He did not see her shoulders tremble. Nor did he see the gold that had just flashed in her left eye, then went out.
Then a piercing pain tore through his throat.
Drag went rigid. Rank blood gushed out, spilling from the corner of his mouth. His eyes flew open. Kristin was still there, pressed against him, so close he could count every one of her ragged breaths. Her eyes were wide, bewildered, as if even she had not yet understood what she had just done. He tried to call her name. No sound came out, only a burning, bleeding hollow inside his mouth.
A chill crawled up his spine. Drag staggered back a step, his vision clouding. But one step was not enough to escape. A second pain struck, more violent, right where her hand had so gently slid past. The knees that had once borne up the whole sky buckled. Drag collapsed. Blood poured onto the bricks.
Drip. Drip.
Kristin stood before him. That look had changed. Colder. Heavier. He told himself it was coldness. He chose to believe that.
She raised two blood-slick fingers to her mouth and slowly pulled out a bright red piece of flesh, still dripping. Drag stared at it blankly. His pride did not yet let him understand. Then he realized.
It was his own tongue.
He wanted to roar an accusation. To know why. But every syllable died choking in the bleeding pit of his throat.
Kristin did not explain. She tried to keep a calm face, but the fingers of her other hand clutched the hem of her shirt until they turned white. He was proud enough to miss it, and hurt enough not to look closely.
Then another pain pierced through, right in the middle of his chest.
A cold mass of metal tore through the muscle and pierced his wildly beating heart. His hand fumbled upward and touched a hilt.
A sword of gold.
-It cannot be-
Dark red blood oozed out, crawling between his stunned fingers. Kristin stepped closer, each step soft, so calm it was frightening.
" Why did you lie? "
Her voice broke on the last word.
Drag raised his clouded eyes to her. He was the one who should be asking that.
Kristin laid her bloodied hand on the hilt, right where that hand had just pressed to listen to his heartbeat. She closed her eyes as she pushed down. He only had time to see her lashes tremble.
The golden blade drove deeper, tearing through every strand of flesh, then struck something sealed away at the very bottom of his chest.
Clang.
A dry, metallic sound echoed from inside him.
Kristin's hand stopped on the hilt. Just for one breath. Then the depths of her eyes darkened, as if she had just found the thing she was most afraid to find.
She leaned down, her lips still stained with blood close beside his ear. Her voice was very soft.
" Why?.. "
Yet so clear and sharp it could not be mistaken, even though the hand on the hilt was still trembling.
" Lucifer. "
