BOOM.
The orb of blood shot straight at Micheal. The impact was so violent that only shattered dust was left drifting through the air.
" A bit slow, aren't you? "
The voice rang out right behind him, light as air, as if the shot had not managed to scratch a single patch of skin.
Drag turned around. A figure light as a feather hung upside down in midair, two fingers pointed straight at him, lips pressed together.
" BOOM. "
A golden ray burst from the fingertips and shot into his back like the light of dawn. It tore clean through him right beside the golden heart, and even the flesh outside its reach was ground away, burned.
The pain struck at once. Drag bit down on his lip, his broken body sinking slowly. Nothing had ever brought him down so fast. The bundles of muscle were burned to their very ends, and they could not regrow as they always had.
Micheal floated gently down from the void. He stood there admiring the sculpture he had carved into the immortal's body, the corner of his mouth curled as if waiting for something. Only when the strands of flesh slowly returned to their old shape did he start to walk. One slow step at a time.
" To Father, you are not perfect. "
Every step that came down was a word ground out between his teeth.
" A prototype for the beginning of mankind. "
He watched intently as the fibers of muscle struggled to weave back together, his contemptuous eyes unmoved.
" Or rather, the disappointment of the heavens. "
Drag pressed both hands against the stone floor.
" Am I right? "
Micheal stamped down hard on the hand still trying to resist. The weight seemed bent on driving his palm deep into the ground. Drag reached up with his other hand to lift the lead block pinning him down, but the golden blade had already swept across in the blink of an eye, leaving only a searing burn at his wrist. Blood welled out as if scorched by molten gold. The hand fell from his body, tumbled across the white floor and caught fire.
This pain was unlike any wound he had ever known. Every cell felt the rot clearly as it worked to eat him away. Drag looked up at the arrogant one before him. The sword in Micheal's hand blazed gold as if cast from the sacred fire of the sun, identical to the sword Drag drew from his own flesh and blood, only the light was different. More complete, cleaner, like the face of the one who held it.
Micheal still had not taken his eyes off the wound on Drag's arm.
" Now I understand why someone like you was cast away. "
The burn at his wrist finally cooled. But what gnawed at him was what had just been said. The words cast away weighed heavy, just like the light that had dragged him from the heavens down to the world below. It seemed Micheal wanted to see more than the wound he had left, something darker, waiting for him to admit it.
Micheal lifted his foot off the filthy hand and swung it straight into the face that looked just like his own. The jaw cracked like rotten wood. Drag's body was flung far away, smashing through a great stretch of wall behind him.
He lurched up from the rubble, wiped the blood from the corner of his mouth, wrenched his jaw back into place and let the immortal blood mend it. He looked at the wrist that still had not regrown. Something was wrong with his curse. But there was no time to think; the freak was strolling toward him.
This time he would not let Micheal strike first. He charged, clenching his remaining fist, drawing a deep breath into his chest. The smoldering black fire flared up again along the red sword.
But before the blade could even rise, an invisible force stopped it. Every cell in his forearm seemed to change masters; a rebellious will crept through his veins, bringing a sense of revulsion with it.
" Lucifer, stab that ugly thing into your own leg. "
The tip of the sword turned on its own toward his calf. The muscles twitched as if trying to resist, but his will was helpless against the betrayal of his own body. The blade went through flesh, with the sound of bone splintering. Blood welled out, then was boiled away by the pitch-black fire itself.
Micheal coughed softly into the back of his hand and wiped it quickly on the hem of his robe before anyone could see.
" How does it feel? "
Drag ground his teeth. For the first time in his endless life, he felt pain born of his own hatred. It was not like the cut of metal, nor the fangs and claws of a beast, but like thousands of vengeful souls screaming, burning the rusted blade in his hand.
Micheal smirked.
" Pain? Anger? Resentment? Which one? "
Broken images surfaced on their own amid the pain. A stretch of dying sky, mountains of innocent dead, banners of conquest planted on an endless sea of people.
" Or regret? "
The memories grew clearer. Dynasties collapsing under his hand, the fear buried deep in the eyes of the living when they beheld the blood sword.
" The feelings the people below once went through. Do you remember? "
Drag lowered his gaze to his hand, still stained with dried blood.
" No one else has to die. "
Micheal leaned down, close to his ear, and whispered.
" Is that what you thought when you met her? "
Each word soft yet clear, boring deep into his rotten self.
" Then why was that blade so merciless? "
Micheal looked at him with blazing red eyes. Drag did not know when he had begun to take that gaze head on. Deep inside the color of blood, he saw only the shadow of a monster out of control, trembling before a truth that could not be denied. He tried to find an excuse convincing enough, that he had never meant that strike for her. But no defense could wash away the filth that came from himself.
His mind slowly clouded over. The hand on the hilt no longer had the strength to pull it from his leg.
Micheal straightened up and pointed two fingers at his heaving chest. Orange-gold sparks gathered at the fingertips.
" Finally ready to give up? "
" This will erase you from the world. Only then can mankind be reborn once more, on a more complete prototype. "
He paused a short beat, then added, more quietly.
" I don't understand why Father pities you so much. I should have been the ideal one for that. "
Each word fell from his mouth heavier than before. For just a moment, Drag realized that Micheal's gaze was no longer on him. It had lifted to some vague emptiness above, as if waiting for something that had never come. The smile had faded at some point. His brows knitted together, and his gaze turned resolute, driving deep into his older brother.
" Goodbye. "
Drag's eyelids shut tight. Could the one before him truly break this eternal curse? He did not know. But if it were so, he was ready to accept death as the last mercy this fragile world could give him. He let out the relieved breath of one who deserved to die. Perhaps the world would be better without this presence.
Suddenly memories flowed through his mind, fast as water running back from the past, to a place where there was only a vast, faraway expanse. From the moment he opened his eyes, the sky had always been like this. Endlessly blue, with no cloud drifting quietly past, and no hand ever touching him.
The scene shifted, leading him to the bloodied blade in his own hand, to people piled upon one another with no breath left in them. He remembered this memory. He remembered the voices roaring a single glorious title. When they won, they praised that name, and when they lost, the last thing to leave their lips was that same name.
-The Blood Horseman-
But the name vanished in a hurry, giving way to an afternoon in Manlanier. The fated day he met that girl. She held his wrist tight and led him through countless strangers.
This scent is always so pleasant, isn't it, he wondered.
In a moment Kristin would point to the Ditovias tavern, he knew it for certain. The corner of his mouth lifted. But this time was different. Kristin raised her hand forward, and her finger did not point toward the tavern. It pointed at his chest. He had never had this memory.
" I believe you can do it. "
The smile on her lips was radiant, her eyes curved into crescents. Before that sight, he smiled too without meaning to, and quickly caught her hand.
Drag's eyes snapped open. The fibers of muscle had wound into a new hand, and at some point he had gripped Micheal's wrist tight. He wrenched it aside.
The ray plunged into the ground, only a hand's breadth from him. The stone burst apart, leaving a bottomless hole that looked straight down to the world below.
Micheal's brows knitted tight, his pupils widening.
" Huh? "
Drag leaned back, drew up both legs, every fiber of muscle taut as a bowstring, and drove a kick straight into Micheal's stomach. Micheal's body slammed into the giant gate pillar behind him, and bricks and stone came down to fill the still silence of the city.
In Drag's hand was still his younger brother's arm, blood clinging to the torn end. On the wrist was a tattoo, a number.
-03-
It did not look like something carved after Micheal appeared. It looked as if it had been there since before he had a form.
Suddenly a golden ray grazed across his shoulder, taking a patch of burning flesh with it as it vanished.
Not far ahead, Micheal was walking closer, his face cold as ice. He wiped the blood on his lips with the back of his hand, then looked at it for a moment, as if looking at a stain that did not belong to him.
" Why don't you just die already? "
Behind the dust of the impact, a brand-new hand was raised. Strands of flesh wound tightly around the pure white bone, steam rising thick from them. Drag froze. It was exactly like the flesh and blood flowing inside him.
" You're that much like me? "
Micheal's steps slowed.
" Like you? "
He looked down at the fingers still warm from being remade.
" Don't be mistaken. This body is whiter, far cleaner than you. "
He looked up and smiled with half of his mouth.
" And more importantly, it carries none of your mistakes. "
Two pairs of wings burst open against the glittering sky, and the two sworn enemies hurled themselves at each other. Sparks of friction trailed ribbons of light in different colors, and black and white feathers hung everywhere in the air. Drag gritted his teeth, bearing the weight of the blade.
" You will never beat me. "
Micheal's mouth curled as he swung again and again, without the slightest mercy. The clash of metal rang across the sky. Drag's sword cracked bit by bit under the lightning assault, the orange-gold flames tearing it to pieces, while on Micheal's face there was not the slightest caution.
Then a shattering sound rang out, shaking the stone floor below. Micheal poured the full strength of a god into the finishing blow. The sword in Drag's hand broke into pieces drifting through the air. In the blink of an eye, Micheal drove his blade through Drag's stomach, fresh blood spilling down to stain the white floor.
" Beat you? Easily. "
The fire on the sword flared up fiercely. Micheal tightened his grip on the hilt and drove it deeper, dragging them both down to the ground. He pinned Drag to the floor, the blade stuck fast as if forbidding him to move, then leaned over and pointed two fingers at his chest.
" I am better than you. Admit it. "
Drag tried to pull the sword from his stomach, but the fire held back his body's healing. The fibers of muscle seemed afraid of that clean light.
" No.. "
This time Micheal's triumphant smile looked strange. Just slightly, as if he were forcing the corners of his mouth to curl.
" Then die. "
Suddenly a silver blade cut through the air toward his back. He spun around.
" Whoooa, so fast! "
The innocent voice rang out from the wreckage of the giant gate. The familiar grubby little girl had finally appeared.
Bel'vi.
She bit her lip at the sight of his face. Micheal needed only one hand to catch the knife she had thrown, gripping it so hard that deep cuts dripped blood onto the floor. What stunned her was not only that he looked exactly like Drag, but the golden light that flickered in his left pupil. Something that should never have been allowed to exist. The Eye of The Ignored Sorrows.
From the wound on his hand, black letters crawled and swarmed, flowing along his veins like the poison of ten thousand grieving voices.
" So the Miracle is ready to evolve the flawed ones below. "
Bel'vi stood frozen at the evolution he spoke of. She clicked her tongue and ran a hand over her forehead.
" Nobody in this family is normal, are they. "
Micheal tilted his head slightly to look at her. Suddenly a red streak grazed past his neck. He still did not take his eyes off the girl, as if he had foreseen that attack long ago.
" If I am not mistaken, you are the daughter of Noah? "
" A serpent carrying a sin that cannot be forgiven. Your father as well. "
A dry voice rang out beneath his feet.
" And you are nothing but my own rot. "
Micheal turned back toward his brother, his brows furrowing at those words. But Drag had already crooked two fingers before he could fully turn. He wanted to strike before Micheal had time to notice. An orb of blood plunged from high above toward the back of his head.
Then the light that flared behind that blazing red eye made Drag freeze for a beat.
-Kristin?-
Micheal simply leaned aside. The orb of blood grazed past and slammed into the broken body lying below, shattering the right shoulder of its armor.
" Rot? "
The strained, twisted smile from before broke apart on his face.
" Someone this pathetic dares talk about rot? "
He used his foot to push the sword deeper, cutting forward.
" You really compare a stain to perfection? Fool. "
Drag tried to stop the blade creeping deeper toward his heart.
" Your perfection is— "
" Shut your mouth, Lucifer. Did I say you could speak? "
Drag's teeth suddenly clamped together, as if an invisible force had ordered him silent for a few moments. Micheal's voice cracked a little as he gave the command. He swallowed, his face giving nothing away.
Micheal studied the dagger still caught in his hand. Blood clung thick to the bright silver blade as he tried to read the lines engraved on it.
" The Whispers of Negated Pains. "
He clicked his tongue, eyes narrowing as if listening to something from the knife itself. His body stilled for a moment, his pupils sinking, lifeless, in his cold face.
" You should vanish from this world, Lucifer. "
His voice dropped, the arrogance from before gone.
" It would be so much easier for all the lives out there. "
" Easier for me too. "
He almost entirely avoided Drag's puzzled gaze.
The moment he finished, Bel'vi rushed toward the two of them. But something seemed faster than her. A shadow streaked past her eyes, then many more shadows hurtled past, leaving only an empty space around where she stood.
The Darksiders had flooded into the city. They swept past her as if she did not exist, as if she had never been their target. The clamor of trampling feet, the beating of wings, the claws cracking into the stone. They charged as if speed had no limit. Micheal whirled around to face the horde of monsters howling and surging toward him.
Golden light flashed, but before he could react, Drag's kick had landed square in his stomach, hurling him into the middle of the monstrous pack.
" The one who once held that thing was far better than you. "
Micheal was surrounded by the hideous creatures, circling and waiting for an opening in their prey. Drag gritted his teeth and pulled the blazing golden sword from his stomach, blood spraying out in ragged splashes. The wound still showed no sign of healing.
Bel'vi was still standing too close to danger. With no time to think, his wings burst open and he flew fast into the gap the Darksiders had left. He caught the girl's wrist with one hand, the other covering the wound still bleeding on his stomach.
Bel'vi pointed at the bloodied hand covering it.
" Why isn't it healing? "
" I think that bastard can kill me. "
" Why do you have so many personal grudges? And a family feud on top of that. "
A little bit of normal again, in a day where nothing was normal anymore. He sighed.
" How would I know? I only met him a moment ago. And why are you only showing up now? "
Bel'vi puffed out her cheeks, pouting in annoyance.
" Do you think that tree is small or something? "
He meant to find a safer place for her. His eyes swept across the towers.
" The monsters don't even look at me. And I can't hear anyone hurting because of them anymore. Something must have happened. "
He did not understand why either, when he flew up into the city, the monsters had helped him attack the heavenly host. Suddenly a building ahead cut his thoughts short. The place was so familiar that a sick unease pounded in his chest.
The Lobby of Dreamless Solitude.
The place where his white wings had once been burned to ash.
Behind him, a horn sounded through the freezing air, followed by a blaring voice. The sound struck his body head on and crept into every corner of it, but it was not loud enough, so it only made his muscles twitch as he strained against it.
" Tear your own mouths open! "
Below, the howling of the monsters suddenly choked into wet, garbled moans.
Bel'vi tugged his shoulder down.
" Did you hear something? I think I heard someone's voice inside you. "
" Something like 'ear 'oor 'ouths o'en. "
She giggled as she said it. He froze for a beat, hoping only that Bel'vi would not be touched by the curse of The Mouth.
" Ridiculous. "
She pinched him.
" You! You're no fun at all. "
He did not feel like answering. Before his wings closed, he glanced down at the edge of the city. Far away, where the field of ash stretched to the horizon, the ground was moving. Not waves, not wind. Tens of thousands of bodies dragging themselves on their bellies, fingers clawing the earth, all heading the same way, toward a giant ancient tree standing pitch black amid the smoke. None of them were allowed to die anymore.
He turned away. His wings wrapped tight around the girl, and he shot straight into the glass dome of the great hall.
---
Cracked glass fell along with what was left of the two wings. He gently folded his ash-grey wings away, and Bel'vi hopped down from his strong frame.
Around them, a circular wall enclosed the vast space. Ancient patterns carved with great care ran all the way around, as if trying to record the history of mankind. Bel'vi spun in a carefree circle amid the magnificent architecture, clean to a frightening degree, nothing at all like the ruined world below.
Suddenly her eyes turned to a kneeling stand, and she scurried over.
" Look. I have a little piece of memory about this place. "
Drag followed in the footsteps she left behind.
" What do you mean? "
Bel'vi crouched before a golden shape carved into the pure white slab. The shape of an ear.
The Ear.
" I don't remember clearly. Just a voice echoing in the void. Only this thing was real. "
Drag quietly looked to the side, not far away. An identical kneeling stand, carved with the shape of lips: The Mouth, the thing Micheal now held. He walked to the last kneeling stand, where the slab carved with an eye had cracked at its center, and slowly sank to one knee. The Eye. The thing Kristin once possessed.
He wanted to know why it was now in Micheal's left eye. Would it pass on to the next chosen ones? He did not know. His hand touched the carved lines on the stone without his meaning to.
" It's so dark! I'm opening the curtains. "
He still had not turned toward the voice that had wandered off at some point.
Bel'vi hurried to pull back the curtain trying to keep out the sunlight. But what lay behind it was not what she expected.
" The Creation of Adam? "
She read the words below aloud before she could stop herself. An old painting done in oils.
" Lucifer, come see what I found! "
Drag finally left the symbol of The Eye and walked over to the girl standing stunned before a strange work. The painting showed a soulless man standing on a mountaintop, his legs seemingly joined to the earth.
" The ancestor of mankind. You remember what I once told you? "
Drag looked at the man's right arm, as if he were reaching for something he could never touch.
" Yes, I remember. "
Bel'vi hurried over to the next curtain and pulled it aside hard to reveal another painting.
" The First Reconstructionism. "
He turned to follow her attention. This one was very familiar, a story Kristin had once carried with her all along the shabby old corridors of the church. Four horsemen, four horses writhing in chaos amid the world of men.
The first horse was a pale white like mist, and so was its rider's armor. In his hand was a bow painted in glittering platinum. Each step the horse took left patches of deadly fog over the rotting bodies of men.
His eyes moved on to the second. This one held a pair of scales, and on the two pans a bundle of wheat was weighed against hundreds of silver coins. The black horse he rode trod step by step, leaving a band of withered yellow across every row of cabbages.
" Look, this horse is the color of a corpse. "
He followed Bel'vi's pointing finger. The rider wore no armor, only a ragged cloth thrown over his body. The horse he rode showed bones jutting out through its ribs. Wherever its hooves passed, black clouds covered the sky.
But what stunned him was the last rider, the one he had not yet noticed. He wore a suit of armor pitch black and dried into the oil paint. The horse seemed deliberately painted in a deep crimson pigment. A familiar color that had flowed from his own body more times than he could count.
Memories suddenly poured into his mind, flashing images he could not see clearly. The rousing roar of soldiers, of endless wars, the deafening clash of metal stained with the color of grieving blood.
He stared at the sword blazing black in the painting. The formless memories came faster. He could not remember whether they were his own or someone else's. The sword drank the blood of the innocent like floodwater.
" Why does he have my sword? "
" Lucifer. Lucifer. "
Her voice pulled him back out of the dark things in his mind.
" Are you okay? "
He turned to look at her. Her face had changed more than before, a little sadness pooling under her lashes that had not yet spilled over. His eyes slid to the next curtain. It had been drawn back at some point without his noticing. He walked closer to the frame where Bel'vi stood rooted, head bowed.
-The Chosen One With The Only Flaw-
The painting showed an old man hanging on a giant cross, surrounded by fierce blue-black waves. The painter seemed to have deliberately put the darkest tones into the thick clouds and the vast, roaring ocean. All but one thing, which was bright.
His smile. Though it was only a small stroke on the canvas, that smile was what made the painting come alive.
" This is my father. "
Her voice came out softly, more downcast than before.
" I can't understand why someone would paint this. He was punished for saving me from a sin I should have carried myself. "
He raised his hand slightly. A word of comfort would be better for the girl. But the hand closed again before it could touch her shoulder. Perhaps it would be better if he just stood still, just stayed beside her right now. He remembered the time she had sat quietly beside him one late afternoon beneath the tree, that day. Neither of them said much, only watched the sky split into two halves. It should be like that now too, he thought.
The vast room fell silent for a while, then a while longer.
" Let's keep looking. "
" Yes. This time I'll open it. "
He pulled the next curtain. The painting revealed was extremely familiar.
" Huh?! "
An angel falling from the sky. The light of the clouds was slowly closing up, dragging dark grey patches across the angel's pure white wings. He knew exactly who this was. He remembered every word he had cursed after that halo of light closed, after what was left of the feathers he once had was burned away.
Suddenly a chill ran down his spine. Patchwork memories crept back. White wings that had once blocked his way, trying to stop him from reaching a giant heart hidden among the towering clouds.
" Mankind needs Reconstruction. "
The voice echoed across the city. But then the memory shut just before it could show him anything more.
" Lucifer, Lucifer? "
Bel'vi's voice pulled him back to reality once again.
" Why do you keep acting like you're possessed? "
She pointed at the painting sitting proudly on the wall.
" Is this you? Why are you always naked? "
He glared at her for a moment. The teasing had become all too familiar to him.
" What's so funny to you? "
Bel'vi covered her mouth and skipped gleefully over to the second-to-last curtain.
" Are you ready? "
She reached up and took hold of the dusty fabric of the curtain, specks of dust glittering in the air under the rare light of the great hall. He walked over slowly to stand beside her.
" They're just paintings about the history of mankind. What is there to be ready for? "
Sensing her hesitation before the curtain, he quickly snatched it from her hand and yanked it aside.
This time the one who hesitated was himself. The thing before him should not exist, and could not exist.
Old brushstrokes left patchy streaks of color on the canvas. Not a single trace suggested this work had been painted recently, from the dust layered on the curtain and the frame to the colors faded by time.
" Kristin? "
The word left his mouth dry and rasping, through a throat full of dust. He wanted to find a possibility, anything at all, to make himself believe the woman lying before him was not her. But that bare tree, the white dress no longer whole that the woman was wearing, only made his heartbeat sag ten thousand times over.
He slowly raised a trembling hand and touched that familiar figure.
-At Hope's Gate-
The woman rested peacefully against the tree. Her eyes were closed, her hand lying limp on the gnarled root. She lay there in the middle of a shattered sky, yet the devastation around her did nothing to stir the dream she was stepping into. The snowflakes of winter and the petals still held her close, as if wanting to warm that fragile body. He saw a kind of contentment on her slack lips.
He bit down hard on his lip. Some part deep inside him ached.
Bel'vi knitted her brows tight.
" Why didn't whoever knew about this stop it? "
Drag did not know who could have foreseen yesterday either. Certainly not Him. That man had never cared about matters so small.
" You don't need to blame someone who didn't cause it. "
" You know very well who should be blamed. "
Bel'vi pressed her lips together and looked at him tenderly.
" There's something I haven't told you about.. "
Before she could finish, the giant door behind them cracked apart, then was blown clean off its hinges by a terrible force.
" Did you really think that filth out there could beat me? "
Micheal strode in proudly, without a single scratch on him after the monsters earlier. His voice had gone hoarse, as if he had just screamed across an entire sky. He looked at the paintings that had been unveiled, showing no surprise at all. His gaze passed over each one, then stopped, for just one beat, at the last curtain still closed.
" Why did you come to this place? "
He turned his gaze toward Drag.
A gaze full of heavy burdens.
